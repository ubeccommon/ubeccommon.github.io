## 1. Purpose

One building dug into a south-facing slope, with the turf carried on
over its roof, that holds a protective shelter for 24 to 30 people, a
cold food store, a winter garden at the entrance with a sauna and a
relax area, and a co-working room for 8 to 10 desks on the floor
above. It is for a mountain site that needs all four and would rather
keep its slope than build four buildings on it.

**Six rules.** The slope stays: the building goes into the hill, and
the turf runs on over both roofs, so that from above only a chimney,
a flue, vents, an air intake, and a hatch show. Water out first: the
drain, the membrane, and the falls are built before anything that
they protect. Warm, cold, and protected kept apart: insulated walls
between the warm rooms and the food store, and the shelter in its own
box behind a wall a metre thick, under undisturbed earth, with
nothing built above it. No straight line from glass to shelter: the
way in is through a lobby with two offset doors. Every door passes a
person 1.80 m tall: at least 0.9 by 2.0 m clear on every main route,
round doors included. Concrete where the earth pushes, stone and
timber where people look.

**What is taken, and what is changed.** The name and three ideas come
from the Earthship of Michael Reynolds and Earthship Biotecture: south
glazing as a buffer, earth as thermal mass, and the building's systems
designed together. Changed here: no tyres, because the shelter and the
food store sit against the earth walls; reinforced concrete where the
earth pushes, because the slope is steep, wet, and loaded with snow;
wood, not the sun, as the winter heat, because Carpathian winters are
cloudy; and drainage before storage, because the problem here is too
much water, not too little. The name is kept, decided 2026-10-06, with
its source named here.

**Connections.** Depends on: a slope survey and a ground investigation;
an engineer, for the structure and the shelter; the wood heat module,
for the co-working stove, the sauna stove, their chimneys, and the hot
water; the energy module, for the shelter's fan and light on the
essential circuit; the water module, for supply; the greywater module,
for the showers and the plunge; the toilets module, for whether the
shelter's WCs are flush or terra preta toilets; the insulation module;
the written enquiries in Section 10. Feeds: the shelter row, with its
structure; the food storage row, with its cellar; the washroom row,
with two shower rooms; the adult strand, with a working room.

**Patterns.** Grows from: not yet named. How homesteads here kept
their cellars and stores, whether a building dug into a slope has a
local form, and where a sauna or bath stood are questions for the
elders [for the elders]. Feeds: not yet named. Resonates with: 168
Connection to the Earth — the building meets the slope by going into
it; 169 Terraced Slope — the two floors step up the hill; 197 Thick
Walls; 128 Indoor Sunlight — the most-used rooms behind the south
glass; 161 Sunny Place — the relax area in the winter garden; 145 Bulk
Storage — the pantry and the root cellar; 230 Radiant Heat — the
masonry stove. In tension with: 104 Site Repair — the cut takes deep,
undisturbed ground to keep the surface whole; the turf comes back, the
ground below does not. The walk status of each is set after the Phase
1 walk, on a later date; until then, unknown (compendium Section 26).

## 3. Sizing method

**What you must measure yourself before using this module:**

- **Your slope** — a levelled profile from the yard to at least 25 m
  up the hill along the building's axis, and two more at its sides;
  by a surveyor, or with a level and a staff.
- **Your ground** — trial pits or boreholes to below the deepest floor,
  with the soil, the rock, and the water in them recorded; a
  geotechnical engineer reads them, including the risk of slides.
- **Your winter sun** — the horizon in December from the position of
  the glass, at midday and two hours either side.
- **Your width** — the width of level ground you can open, with a metre
  of working space at each side.
- **Your radon** — a two-month test in any existing cellar nearby, and
  again in the building before it is lived in.

### 3.1 The slope sets the building

The ground behind the bank is taken as rising 0.6 m for every metre
back, about 31°, [assumed]; the floor of the lower floor and the
shelter is at the yard's level.

| Quantity | Calculation | Our example |
|---|---|---|
| Ground over a point y m back | 2.0 + 0.6 × y | — |
| Earth over the shelter | ground at y 14.5 − roof at 3.6 | about 7 m |
| Back face of the cut | ground at y 19.8 + 0.4 m | about 14.3 m |
| Escape shaft, floor to hatch | ground at y 19.8 − 3.0 m | about 10.9 m |
| Excavation, bank volume | Section 3.3 | about 5,200 m³ |

**This is the finding of the draft.** On a slope this steep, each metre
the shelter goes back into the hill adds 0.6 m to the cut, the cover,
and the shaft. A 7 m cover is more than a protective structure may
need, and a 14 m back cut cannot stand open in most ground without
support. The shelter can sit further forward, under less cover, with
a shorter shaft; the escape route can leave sideways onto the slope
instead of climbing a shaft; or both. The engineer's cover and the
measured slope decide it, [set].

### 3.2 The shelter

| Quantity | Calculation | Our example |
|---|---|---|
| Floor area, inside | 14.0 × 7.0 m | 98 m² |
| Floor area a person | 98 ÷ 30 people | about 3.3 m² |
| Volume a person | 98 × 3.0 m ÷ 30 | about 9.8 m³ |
| Clear height | as drawn | 3.0 m |
| Bunks, two tiers | 30 ÷ 2 = 15; drawn 16 | 16, 32 berths |
| Bunks, three tiers | 30 ÷ 3, by the norm | 10 or 11 |
| Air, clean ventilation | the norm's rate a person | [held] |

**The norm.** ДБН В.2.2-5:2023 asks at least 2.5 m of clear height in
new construction (clause 7.1.5), and two-tier bunks up to 2.9 m and
three-tier above it (clause 7.1.6), as read on 2026-10-06. At 3.0 m
the drawings' two-tier bunks fall under the three-tier rule: the
engineer either lowers the room to 2.9 m or fits three tiers, which
frees about a third of the bunk floor. The minimum area and volume a
person — 0.5 m² with bunks and 1.5 m³ — are taken from a secondary
source, a conference paper, and are read in the norm before they are
relied on; the design gives about six times the area, because it is meant
for days, not hours.

**The supply.** Stored water, light, the fan's power, and sanitation
for the people the shelter holds are sized from the norm and from the
energy module's essential circuit, and are kept in the workshop
(compendium Section 2). The method: people × the norm's daily rate ×
the days the norm sets; the fan by the norm's rate a person in each
mode, with a hand drive.

**Ways out.** The way in, through the hall and a lobby with two
protective doors that are never in line; a second lobby into the root
cellar, so that food is reached without leaving; and an escape route
through the north wall, by a short tunnel to a shaft with a ladder and
a hatch that opens from inside. The norm's requirements for exits, the
hatch, and its distance from buildings are read before the route is
fixed, [set].

### 3.4 Doors

The rule: at least 0.9 m clear width and 2.0 m clear height across
that width on every main route, so that a person 1.80 m tall, a
wheelchair, or a trolley of crates passes; 0.8 m for the sauna. A
round door is a circle of radius r whose centre stands c above the
floor, so that the floor cuts it:

| Quantity | Calculation | D1, r 1.25, c 1.1 | D2, D3, r 1.15, c 1.0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Height | c + r | 2.35 m | 2.15 m |
| Width at the floor | 2 × √(r² − c²) | 1.19 m | 1.14 m |
| Width at 2.0 m | 2 × √(r² − (2.0 − c)²) | 1.73 m | 1.14 m |
| 0.9 × 2.0 m box, spare | r − corner distance | 62 mm | 53 mm |

A plain circle standing on the floor is too narrow at the feet; the
cut circle is what lets a round door pass people. The spare is at the
structural opening, for the frame and the rebate. In the first sketches
D1 was 2.4 m across, with 12 mm to spare, and D2 and D3 were 2.2 m,
with 3 mm; they are 2.5 m and 2.3 m here for that reason, decided
2026-10-06. The door schedule,
sheet 4, draws every door with a person 1.80 m tall in it.

### 3.5 Heat, light, and the cold

The winter garden is an unheated buffer between the yard and the
hall. The co-working room and the wellness room are heated by the wood
heat module's appliances: a masonry stove on the co-working room's back
wall and a wood sauna stove fired from the utility room, each with its
own flue; their heat loads are calculated when the module's buildings
are, [set]. The food store keeps cold by the earth behind it and two
vents, behind an insulated wall; the pantry is held at 8 to 14 °C and
the root cellar at 1 to 5 °C, [set], as targets for the food storage
module to confirm.

## 7. Operation and maintenance

Responsible: one named adult for the building and one for the shelter,
each with a deputy — [from L2]. What the design already asks:

- **Monthly:** every protective door closed and opened, its seal looked
  at; the hatch opened from inside; the fan turned by hand.
- **Before each winter:** the drains and their outlets clear; the vents
  of the food store open and screened; the glazing's seals sound.
- **After heavy snow:** the glazing clear and the top vents free; the
  hatch and the air intake not buried.
- **Each spring:** the turf on the roofs checked for wet patches, which
  are the first sign of a failed membrane below.
- **Each year:** the shelter's readiness checked as the rules for
  protective structures set, [set]; the radon test repeated.

## 11. Adaptation notes

The design is a seed, not a copy: a different place grows a different
form from it.

- **A gentler slope:** less cover over the shelter; the berm built up
  over it, and the upper floor set further back.
- **A steeper slope:** a deeper cut for the same floor; the shelter
  forward and the escape route out to the side; a shorter building.
- **Rock near the surface:** less support, more breaking; the shelter
  where the rock lets it, not where the drawing puts it.
- **Wetter ground:** a drainage blanket behind every buried wall, a
  heavier membrane, and the lowest floor raised.
- **A colder valley:** thicker insulation, less glass on the upper
  floor, and a larger stove.
- **A smaller group:** a shelter half the width, two-tier bunks in a
  lower room, the food store reached the same way.
- **No shelter needed:** no separation wall, lobbies, or protective
  works; a cellar behind the food store instead; about half the
  concrete.
- **Water for the shelter:** stored as the water module and the norm
  set, whatever the site's own sources.

## 13. What to check

- **The slope** — surveyed; every quantity in Section 3 recalculated.
- **The ground** — trial pits and a geotechnical report; the slide risk;
  how the back cut is supported, and its cost.
- **The cover and the escape route** — the engineer's cover for the
  shelter; whether it moves forward, and whether the route leaves
  sideways instead of by a shaft.

  rate in kg a m³ with it.

  volume a person, the exits, the hatch, the supply, and the air read
  in ДБН В.2.2-5:2023; two or three tiers of bunks.
