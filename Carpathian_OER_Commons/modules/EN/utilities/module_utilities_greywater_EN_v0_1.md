*Converted from the sanitation working paper, EN v0.2, Sections 6, 7,
8.5, and 8.6. Three things change from the paper, decided on
2026-09-29: the reed bed is sized at 5 m² a person, not 4; every used

## 1. Purpose

Every used water on the site — showers, basins, the kitchen, the two
existing flush toilets, and the rinse of the toilet buckets — made
clean enough to return to the ground: a grease trap, a septic tank, a
planted gravel bed, and a soakaway, all by gravity, working under snow.

**Five rules.** Nothing raw reaches the bed: the kitchen passes a
grease trap, and all used water settles in the septic tank first.
Below the surface: the water in the bed stays 5 to 15 cm under the
gravel, so there is no standing water, little smell, and nothing to
reach. Gravity before pumps: the chain runs downhill, from building to
tank to bed to soakaway. Leave the reeds standing: they are cut in
spring, not autumn, because their litter keeps the bed working in
winter. The ground decides the outflow: the soakaway is sized by a
percolation test, and the pond is built only where the authorities
allow it and its distances are met.

**Connections.** Depends on: the water module, whose used water this
is; the toilets module, whose two flush toilets carry about a third of
uses; the terra preta module, whose bucket rinse goes to the septic
tank; the washroom block and the kitchen and dining modules, for the
showers, basins, and kitchen; the Phase 1 walk, with the fall of the
ground, the depth to groundwater, a percolation test, and every
distance in Section 4 measured; the written enquiries in Section 10.
Feeds: the ground, through the soakaway; or, where permitted, the pond.

**Patterns.** Grows from: not yet named. Where the valley let its used
water go, and whether any wet ground was kept for it, are questions for
the elders. Feeds: not yet named. Resonates with: 104 Site Repair —
tank, bed, and soakaway on the poorer ground, below the buildings; 172
Garden Growing Wild — the reed bed as a wild edge, left to itself but
for its spring cut; 25 Access to Water — every part of it outside the
belt along the stream and the pond. In tension with: 64 Pools and
Streams — the soakaway returns the water out of sight, where the paper's
pond would have left a visible end; 71 Still Water — the pond, if it is
built, is a treatment pond, fenced and shallow at its edges, never a
place to swim.

## 3. Sizing method

**What you must measure yourself before using this module:**

- **Your flow** — the water used on a full day at capacity, from the
  water meter or the cistern's fall, and the kitchen's share of it.
- **Your fall** — the levels of building, tank, bed, and soakaway, from
  the walk.
- **Your soil** — a percolation test where the soakaway would go, and
  the depth to groundwater in the wettest month.
- **Your distances** — as in Section 4, measured with a tape.

**Our example:** capacity, 34 people; greywater_per_person, 55 L a
day, [assumed]; toilet_uses, 5 a day; bucket_share, two-thirds;
flush_volume, 5 L, [assumed]; meals_per_day, 3, [assumed];
reed_bed_area, 5 m² a person; soil_loading, 16 to 24 L a m² a day.
Values from the site parameters file, held in the workshop.

### 3.1 The flow

| Stream | Calculation | Our example |
|---|---|---|
| Showers, basins, kitchen | 34 × 55 L | about 1,870 L |
| Flush toilets, uses | 34 × 5 × one-third | about 57 |
| Flush toilets, water | 57 × 5 L | about 283 L |

| **A day at capacity** | sum | **about 2.2 m³** |

The water module sizes the spring for 75 L a person a day, 2.55 m³ at
capacity; about 2.2 m³ of it returns as used water. Greywater from
basic, piped settings runs from 20 to 30 L a person a day where water
is scarce to 90 to 120 where it is not (Morel and Diener, Greywater
Management in Low and Middle-Income Countries, Eawag, 2006); the paper's
55 L is kept, [assumed], until the meter replaces it.

### 3.2 The parts

| Part | Calculation | Our example |
|---|---|---|
| Grease trap | EN 1825-2, meal method | nominal size about 0.5 |
| Septic tank | 3 days × 2.2 m³ | about 6.5 m³ |
| First chamber | 0.75 of the volume | about 4.9 m³ |
| Second chamber | 0.25 of the volume | about 1.6 m³ |
| Reed bed | 34 × 5 m² | 170 m² |
| Gravel in the bed | 170 m² × 0.7 m | about 119 m³ |
| Soakaway base | 2.2 m³ ÷ 16–24 L a m² | about 88–132 m² |
| Soakaway trench, 0.6 m wide | base ÷ 0.6 m | about 147–220 m |

**The grease trap.** EN 1825-2 sizes a grease separator from meals, a
volume a meal, and a peak factor; with the works-canteen values of 5 L
a meal and a factor of 20, as given in a manufacturer's sizing sheet
(Zehnder), 102 meals a day over 8 hours give a flow of about 0.35 L a
second, and a nominal size of about 0.5 where the water is under 60 °C.
The kitchen's own count sets it, [set].

**The septic tank.** ДБН В.2.5-75:2013, clause 10.8.3: septic tanks
for flows up to 20 m³ a day; a volume of at least three days' flow up
to 5 m³ a day; two chambers up to 10 m³ a day, divided 0.75 and 0.25.
The paper's tank took the flush toilets only; here all used water
settles before the bed, because primary treatment is essential to a
subsurface bed (Tilley and colleagues, Compendium of Sanitation Systems
and Technologies, 2014), and greywater carries hair, lint, and fat that
would clog its inlet.

**The reed bed.** Horizontal subsurface flow, lined. DWA-A 262 (2017)
gives 5 m² a person for horizontal-flow beds, at least 20 m², and a
filter layer of at least 50 cm, as reported by German planning sources;
Tilley and colleagues give 5 to 10 m². The paper's 4 m² a person is
below both; 5 m² is used. Saturated depth about 0.6 m, with 0.1 m of
dry gravel above the water; gravel of 5 to 10 mm in the bed and 50 to
100 mm at the inlet and outlet zones (Wafler, Small-Scale Constructed
Wetlands, seecon, 2008); the water level held 5 to 15 cm below the
surface by an adjustable outlet; a liner of at least 1 mm (DWA-A 262);
reeds planted at about four a m² in early spring (UN-Habitat,
Constructed Wetlands Manual, 2008).

**The soakaway.** Filtration trenches take up to 15 m³ a day, in sandy,
sandy-loam, or loamy soil taking at least 0.1 m a day (ДБН
В.2.5-75:2013, clause 10.8.2). The loading is taken from a soils table
for septic-tank effluent: 0.4 to 0.6 US gallons a square foot a day
for loam, sandy loam, and silt loam, by structure (Tyler, Hydraulic
Wastewater Loading Rates to Soil, University of Wisconsin), about 16 to
24 L a m² a day. The bed's outflow is cleaner than septic effluent and
the same table allows it more; that allowance is not used here. The
percolation test replaces the range; the upper length is costed.

**Winter.** A subsurface bed keeps working under snow; beds without
snow cover have frozen and failed in a severe winter, and 15 cm of
mulch over a 5 cm air gap kept one working at −28 °C (Wallace, Parkin,
and Cross, Water Science and Technology, 2001). The reed litter is left
standing as the paper holds; a mulch is added if the first winter shows
the bed freezing, [set].

No distance is set for a reed bed in the code found; the soakaway's
25 m is used until the enquiry answers. The soakaway also stays outside
the stream's water protection zone, which is wider than the strip, and
in which filtration fields are not allowed (Section 10). If the pond
counts as a biological pond under the code, its distance from housing
is 200 m.

**The layout, as designed.** Building, grease trap, septic tank,
distribution chamber, reed bed, and soakaway in a line down the fall,
each below the last; the reed bed on poorer ground, lined, level across,
fed across its full width; the soakaway downslope of everything and
upslope of nothing that is drunk. Drawn as relationships only; the walk
sets the rest.

**The pond, as the option.** The paper's pond: about 25 m², shallow,
lined, planted, fenced with a gate, shallow at its edges, taking only
the bed's outflow. A pond of this kind gives little treatment in the
cold months and needs mosquito control (SSWM, Waste Stabilisation
Ponds); it is a habitat and a visible end, not a treatment step, and
is built only where the distance and the permit in Section 10 are met.
The paper costed it at 37,200 UAH at 25 people — liner, excavation,
fence and gate — outside the total below.

## 7. Operation and maintenance

Responsible: one named adult, paid, with a deputy — [from L2].

- **Weekly:** the grease trap cleaned, its fat to the terra preta
  vessels in small measure.
- **Monthly:** the inspection points checked for water at the surface;
  the outlet level set.
- **Quarterly:** the septic tank inspected; its scum and sludge
  measured.
- **Spring:** the reeds cut, never in autumn; the soakaway's ground
  walked for wet patches.
- **Every two to five years:** the septic tank desludged.

How the programme relates to this work is described in the programme
modules, in standing format.

## 11. Adaptation notes

The design is a seed, not a copy: a different place grows a different
form from it.

- **A drier valley:** less greywater; the bed and soakaway shrink with
  the flow; keep the reeds watered in drought.
- **A colder valley:** mulch the bed from the first winter; lay the
  tank and pipes below the frost line.
- **A smaller group:** the bed at 5 m² a person, never below 20 m²; the
  septic tank at three days' flow.
- **A larger group:** above 10 m³ a day, three septic chambers; above
  15 m³, no soakaway under the code found here.
- **Flush toilets throughout:** the flow rises; recalculate the tank,
  the bed, and the soakaway.
- **Impermeable soil:** no soakaway; the pond with its permit, or what
  the authorities name.
