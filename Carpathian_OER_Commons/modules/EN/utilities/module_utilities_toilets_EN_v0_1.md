## 1. Purpose

Toilets for everyone on site at capacity that need no water, no pipe,
and no power to work: a seat over a lidded bucket in a warm, private,
lit cubicle, a scoop of char and carbon cover after every use, a vent,
and handwashing at the door. Simple where it is handled; everything
difficult happens afterwards, in the terra preta module.

**Five rules.** A bucket under a seat: no separator, no moving part, no
plumbing; urine goes in with the rest, as decided on 2026-09-29. Char
and cover at every use: a scoop from the bin beside the seat, so that
no deposit is left uncovered. Air out, not in: a dark vent pipe from
the bucket chamber above the roof, with a fly screen and a small fan.
Hands washed at the door: soap, water, and a towel within reach of
every cubicle. Clean twice a day at least, by a named adult, paid, with
a written procedure; toloka is not the name for this work.

**Connections.** Depends on: the biochar module for char, and the
terra preta module for the cover it sizes; the energy module for the
fans on the essential circuit, or a small panel of their own; the
washroom block module for washbasins and hot water; the written answer
of the district sanitary service on whether bucket toilets are accepted
for the children's side. Feeds: the terra preta module, with the full
buckets every day. Beside it: the two existing flush toilets, which
carry about a third of uses and drain to the septic tank of the
greywater module.

**Patterns.** Grows from: not yet named. How homesteads in this valley
kept a privy, and where it stood, are questions for the elders; the
literature found nothing. Feeds: not yet named. Resonates with: 178
Compost — toilets that feed the soil, through the terra preta module,
for the trees; 127 Intimacy Gradient — the cubicles off the common
rooms, private and reached without passing through them; 36 Degrees of
Publicness — a unit for staff and visitors apart from the children's
side. In tension with: 129 Common Areas at the Heart — the toilets near
the shared rooms, never opening onto them; 120 Paths and Goals — the
path from the sleeping rooms to the toilets at night, lit and short,
with the fire protection and night lighting module.

## 3. Sizing method

**What you must measure yourself before using this module:**

- **Your uses** — uses a person a day and the share the flush toilets
  take, from the first sessions.
- **Your cover** — how much a use takes to leave no smell, from the
  first week.
- **Your building** — where the cubicles can stand inside the heated
  envelope, with a vent route to the roof.
- **Your rules** — the written answer of the sanitary service.

**Our example:** capacity, 34 people; toilet_uses, 5 a day;
bucket_share, two-thirds; urine_per_person, 1.4 L; faeces_per_person,
0.128 kg; char_at_seat, 0.5 L; cover_at_seat, 0.8 L, [assumed]. Values
from the site parameters file, held in the workshop.

| Quantity | Calculation | Our example |
|---|---|---|
| Uses at the buckets, a day | 34 × 5 × two-thirds | about 113 |
| Uses a cubicle, a day | 113 ÷ 6 | about 19 |
| Bucket volume, a day | the terra preta module, 3.1 | about 64 L |
| A bucket, a day | 64 L ÷ 6 | about 11 L |
| Char a use | 11.3 L ÷ 113 | about 0.1 L |
| Cover a use | 18.1 L ÷ 113 | about 0.16 L |
| Char and cover, a seat a day | 29.4 L ÷ 6 | about 4.9 L |
| A 60 L bin lasts | 60 L ÷ 4.9 L | about 12 days |
| Buckets | 6 in use, 6 clean | 12 of 30 L |

A 30 L bucket exchanged daily carries about 11 L, and never gets heavy
enough to be a lifting problem. The paper's two 30 L buckets changed
daily at 25 people counted faeces and char only; with urine and cover
the load rises, and daily exchange of every bucket keeps it light.

**Six cubicles, and why.** The paper has four on the children's side,
split between boys and girls, one for staff and visitors, and one that
is accessible. The repealed sanitary rules for children's recreation
establishments gave one toilet bowl for every 15 to 20 users
(ДСанПіН 5.5.5.23-99, point 5.5); six cubicles and the two flush
toilets are well inside that at capacity. What the 2026 regulation asks
is for the enquiry to confirm (Section 10).

**The vent.** Jenkins holds that cover of the right kind, in the right
amount, makes a toilet odourless "without the need for venting"
(Humanure Handbook, cover material guidance). The repealed rules gave
toilets their own exhaust ventilation (point 5.7), and the 2026 rules
are not yet read in full; so each cubicle keeps a vent — a pipe of at
least 110 mm, rising more than 300 mm above the roof, painted dark,
with an aluminium fly screen of 1.2 to 1.5 mm mesh (Tilley and
colleagues, Compendium of Sanitation Systems and Technologies, 2014) —
and a 12 V fan.

**The cubicles, as designed.** Warm, private, and lit, inside the
building; opaque walls and doors; washable surfaces; a seat box with a
hinged front for the bucket, the lid closing over it; the bin of char
and cover beside the seat with its scoop; the vent from the bucket
chamber; handwashing at the door. The children's side split between
boys and girls; a unit for staff and visitors apart.

**The accessible cubicle.** To ДБН В.2.2-40:2018 with its Amendment 2,
as summarised by the ministry: at least 1.65 × 1.8 m in a
reconstruction, 1.7 × 2.2 m new; a door of 0.9 m without threshold,
opening outward; two grab rails 70 to 75 cm apart, one of them
fold-down; a seat 40 to 48 cm high; an alarm within reach from the
seat and from the floor.

**An outdoor unit, if one is used for the working day.** It keeps the
distances the regulation gives outdoor toilets — at least 50 m from
sleeping buildings and the dining hall, as reported — and those of the
compost and terra preta modules from the spring, the stream, and the
pond. None has been measured.

## 7. Operation and maintenance

Responsible: one named adult, paid, with a deputy — [from L2]. Not a
rota.

- **At every use:** a scoop of char and cover over the deposit; the lid
  closed.
- **Twice a day at least:** cubicles cleaned with detergent and
  disinfectant, seats, handles, and floors included, and the cleaning
  recorded; bins topped up; paper, soap, and towels checked.
- **Daily, in session:** every bucket exchanged by the responsible
  adult, as the terra preta module describes, and a clean bucket set
  in with a layer of cover in its base.
- **Weekly:** vent screens and fans checked; bins refilled from the
  store.
- **Seasonal:** vent pipes cleared; roof sleeves checked.

The responsible adult wears gloves and is trained in the written
procedure, as WHO asks of anyone who handles excreta (Guidelines on
Sanitation and Health, 2018). How the programme describes the toilets
to those who use them is for the programme modules, in standing format.

## 8. Failure modes

From the design, not from experience.

| Failure | Early sign | Repair and cost |
|---|---|---|
| Smell in the cubicle | smell | more cover; fan checked |
| Flies | flies at the seat | cover; screen; lid closed |
| Bucket too full | weight | exchange more often |
| Bin empty | [from L2] | refill daily in session |
| Fan stops | no draught | power; spare fan, 816 UAH |
| Vent leaks at roof | stain | sleeve resealed, [from L2] |
| Toilets refused | written answer | the plumbed fallback |

The paper names the four questions an inspector will ask — smell,
flies, hand hygiene, and what happens to the material — and answers
them with the cover, the vent, handwashing at the door, and the terra
preta module's written procedure and rule of the year.

## 11. Adaptation notes

The design is a seed, not a copy: a different place grows a different
form from it.

- **A drier valley:** unchanged; the toilets use no water.
- **A colder valley:** the cubicles stay inside the heated envelope;
  vent pipes insulated where they cross cold roof space.
- **A smaller group:** fewer cubicles, keeping a unit for staff and
  visitors and an accessible one.
- **A larger group:** more cubicles at the ratio your rules give; the
  terra preta module's vessels scale with them.
- **Plumbed units required:** the bucket cubicles serve staff, guests,
  and the working day; the children's side extends the flush toilets.
- **No power:** the vent runs on draught alone, dark and tall; the fan
  is an aid, not a condition.
