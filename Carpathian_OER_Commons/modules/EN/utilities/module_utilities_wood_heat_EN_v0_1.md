*Maturity: **L0**, a marked draft — a sizing method and a design,
published so that they can be read and changed in the open. The
buildings the heat serves have not been surveyed, so no heat load,
boiler size, or year's firewood is given for the site; the priced lines
are the working papers' ranges for autumn 2026, and the chimneys, the
woodshed, the firewood, and the safety fittings are not priced. Read
every figure below at this level and no higher. The module moves to L1
when the buildings are surveyed, the heat load is calculated for each,
and every line in Section 6 carries a dated cost and its basis.*

*Governed by Section 2 of the compendium. No child appears in this
module in any form. The site is not named; site plans are schematic;
photographs show structures and systems only. Design loads are site
capacity, used for sizing only.*

*Converted from the energy working paper, EN v0.1, Section 8, and the
sanitation working paper, EN v0.2, Sections 7 and 8.7, the water
heater and the solar collectors. Five things change from the papers,
decided on 2026-10-03: every wood fire on the site is in this module,
the cookstove and the masonry heater as appliances, with the chimneys
and the firewood for all of them; the buffer tank is sized by rule, and
carries the sleeping rooms' heat after the fire rules put the fire out
two hours before sleep; the solar loop is pumped, never a water-filled
thermosiphon; firewood is bought and priced, never counted as the
household's contribution of labour, and the site's own wood enters
only on a felling ticket, by paid hands; and gravity circulation is
preferred, so that the heat does not wait on electricity.*

*Permits and local rules. This module is a guide, used in different
regions and countries under different laws and guidelines. Depending on
where it is built, at what scale, and for whom, permits, registrations,
or approvals may be required — for building, water, sanitation, fire,
waste, food, or the use of forest and land. Section 10 records what was
found for Ukraine, dated, and unchecked until signed; a reader
elsewhere checks their own rules with the competent authorities before
building, and nothing here replaces that.*

## 1. Purpose

Warmth for the sleeping accommodation and the shared rooms, and hot
water for the washroom and the kitchen, from wood and the sun, through
a mountain winter and through power cuts. A log boiler into a buffer
tank, radiators fed by gravity, a masonry heater in the gathering
place, a cookstove in the kitchen, a small water heater and solar
collectors for the summer, and a year's firewood dried under a roof.

**Six rules.** No heat from electricity: wood and the sun carry the
heat, and the energy module carries none of it. Burn hot, store the
heat: a log boiler burns at full output into a buffer tank, never
throttled, because a smouldering fire makes tar and smoke. The fire
out before sleep, the warmth kept: where people stay round the clock,
firing ends two hours before sleep, and the buffer and the masonry
heater carry the night. Gravity before pumps: heat moves from boiler
to buffer to radiators by gravity where the buildings allow it. Dry
wood only: under 20 percent moisture, split, a year ahead. Bought or
felled by right: firewood is bought and priced, or felled on a ticket
by paid hands; the deadwood stays in the forest, and no heat depends
on any work the programme does.

**Connections.** Depends on: the buildings modules — sleeping
accommodation, the kitchen and dining, the covered gathering place and
hearth, insulation — for the heat load of each building, and the
kitchen and the gathering place for whether and where a fire stands in
their rooms; the Phase 1 building survey; the water module, for cold
water and its pressure; the energy module, for the solar loop's pump
and any controls; the written enquiries in Section 10. Feeds: the
washroom block, with hot water from the tank outlet on; the kitchen,
with hot water and its cookstove; the sleeping accommodation and the
shared rooms, with heat. Takes from: the micro-hydro module's dump
load, as an option.

**Patterns.** Grows from: not yet named. How the stove stood to the
house, whether an open fire belongs in the gathering place beside a
masonry heater or instead of it, where the woodpile stands, and which
wood is not burned are questions for the elders; the literature
records a cooking ledge with an open fire beside the stove, and a
firewood shed against the house [for the elders]. Feeds: not yet
named. Resonates with: 230 Radiant Heat — the masonry heater and the
radiators warm by radiation, with a seat against the heater's warm
side; 145 Bulk Storage — a year's wood kept dry under a roof, ahead of
its burning; 128 Indoor Sunlight — every room on the south asks less
wood; 83 Master and Apprentices — paid hands beside the stove-builder.
In tension with: 181 The Fire — the fire rules end firing two hours
before sleep where people stay round the clock, and a boiler in its
own room hides its fire; the masonry heater keeps a fire at the centre
by day and its heat by night. 7 The Countryside and 172 Garden Growing
Wild — wood is taken only where tenure and a felling ticket allow, and
the deadwood stays. 105 South Facing Outdoors — the solar collectors
share the south roof with the array. The walk status of each is set
after the Phase 1 walk, on a later date; until then, unknown
(compendium Section 26).

## 3. Sizing method

**What you must measure yourself before using this module:**

- **Your buildings** — each one's heated floor, walls, roof, windows,
  and how it is heated now, from a survey; its heat loss at the design
  temperature, calculated by EN 12831 or by an engineer.
- **Your hot water** — the hot side's meter over a full day at
  capacity, and the temperature of the cold water at the tap.
- **Your wood** — what is bought, by the stacked cubic metre, and its
  moisture, with a moisture meter on a freshly split face.
- **Your roof for the collectors** — its pitch, its aspect, and the
  share the array needs.

**Our example:** design_temperature, −20 °C; heating_season, 175 to
250 days; capacity, 34 people; hot_water_per_person, 40 L a day at 45
°C, [assumed]; use_days_per_year, 120; boiler_efficiency, 0.74 to
0.81; firewood_energy, 1,333 to 1,885 kWh a stacked m³; buffer_per_kw,
55 to 100 L a kW; exchange_rate, 52 UAH to the euro. Values from the
site parameters file, held in the workshop.

### 3.1 The heat load

The heat load of a building is its heat loss at the design
temperature, in kilowatts. Before a survey it can only be bracketed: a
German rule-of-thumb table gives 157 to 163 W a m² of heated floor for
an uninsulated building from before 1977, falling to 36 to 38 W a m²
for one built since 2009 (Reonic, 2026), at German design temperatures
that are milder than −20 °C. That is 3.6 to 16.3 kW for each 100 m² of
heated floor, a plausibility check and no more; the survey and the
calculation replace it, building by building, [set].

A new or renovated building here is held to ДБН В.2.6-31:2021, in force
since 01.09.2022, which sets a thermal resistance of at least 4.0 m²·K
a W for external walls in temperature zone I; the Carpathian oblasts
are given as zone I by secondary sources, to be confirmed on the
standard's own map. The insulation module carries it.

### 3.2 The firewood

| Quantity | Calculation | Each 10,000 kWh of heat |
|---|---|---|
| Beech, stacked m³ | 10,000 ÷ (1,885 × 0.74–0.81) | about 6.5–7.2 |
| Spruce, stacked m³ | 10,000 ÷ (1,333 × 0.74–0.81) | about 9.3–10.1 |

The heat a year is each building's heat load, its hours of heating,
and its use, from the survey; until then the firewood is given per
10,000 kWh of heat delivered. The energy figures are at 20 percent
water content, with 0.7 solid cubic metres to the stacked (LWF
Merkblatt 12, 2024); Ukrainian sales count the складометр, and ГОСТ
3243-88 gives 0.63 to 0.72 solid to stacked by kind and split. The
efficiency is what log boilers gave in use, 74 percent gross and 81
net, about fifteen points below the laboratory (Kiwa, for BEIS, 2019).

**Drying.** Split and covered, wood outdoors reached 20 percent in
about six months and about 15 after nine; in poorer conditions it took
a year or more, and unsplit wood longer (LWF and TFZ, drying trials,
2006). Stored wood loses up to about 3 percent of its heat a year, and
wood left open re-wets to near 30 percent in autumn. So a year's wood
is bought or felled a year ahead, split, and kept under a roof that
covers its top, off the ground, with the wind through it.

### 3.3 The boiler and the buffer

| Quantity | Calculation | Each 10 kW of boiler |
|---|---|---|
| Buffer, the legal minimum | 55 L × kW | 550 L |
| Buffer, one fill a day | about 100 L × kW | about 1,000 L |
| Buffer, by fuel chamber | 12 L × the chamber's litres | [set] |
| Heat held, 85 to 60 °C | 1,000 L × 1.163 Wh × 25 K | about 29 kWh |

**The boiler.** A log gasification boiler, sized to the heat load of
the buildings it serves at the design temperature, [set]. It burns at
full output into the buffer and is never throttled to keep the fire
in: a buffer too small makes the boiler choke its air, and tar forms
in the fuel chamber (Koop, haustec, 2022). Each start and stop cost
the equivalent of one to five hours of steady running in the field
trial (Kiwa, for BEIS, 2019). The return water is held above 55 to 60
°C by a thermal mixing valve, or tar and condensate corrode the boiler.

**The buffer.** The German rule for a hand-fed boiler is at least 12 L
of buffer for each litre of fuel chamber and never less than 55 L a kW
(1. BImSchV, §5, as given in BDH Infoblatt 22, 2011); for one fill a
day, about 100 L a kW (Bavarian LfU). One boiler of this kind, 15 kW
with an 80 L chamber, asks 1,000 L (Fröling, S1 Turbo data sheet).

**The night.** The fire rules end firing two hours before sleep where
people stay round the clock (Section 10). The buffer carries the
sleeping rooms through the night: a building that loses P kilowatts at
the night's temperature asks P × the night's hours in kilowatt-hours,
and each 1,000 L of buffer between 85 and 60 °C holds about 29 kWh, the
range gravity radiators can use. The buffer is sized for the larger of
the boiler's rule and the night's need, [set] from the survey.

### 3.4 Gravity circulation

Gravity moves the water because hot water is lighter: about 6.4 mbar
of driving pressure for 5 m of height between the boiler's middle and
the radiators' at 90 and 70 °C (Wikipedia, Schwerkraftheizung). The
installer guides found ask for the radiators about 2 m or more above
the boiler, mains of no more than about 25 m to the furthest riser, a
slope of at least 5 mm a metre toward the boiler, and pipes much larger
than a pumped system's (Klimosz; Ukrainian installer guides). The open
expansion vessel stands at the highest point, at least 0.7 m above the
highest radiator, with no valve of any kind on its safety and
expansion pipes, the safety pipe at least 8.08 × √kW mm and never under
25 mm, all insulated where the room can fall to 0 °C (PN-B-02413:1991,
as taught at Wrocław University of Science and Technology).

Where a building does not allow it, the radiators are pumped, and the
boiler keeps a gravity heat leak — a radiator on a pipe with no pump,
for about a quarter of the boiler's output (Solid Fuel Association) —
so that a power cut never leaves a full firebox with nowhere to put
its heat. A thermal discharge valve, which opens at about 95 °C and
runs cold water through a coil in the boiler to the drain, needs mains
pressure to work (Watts, STS data sheet); the water module's gravity
supply gives about 1 to 2 bar, so whether it is enough is checked with
the valve's maker.

### 3.5 Hot water

| Quantity | Calculation | Our example |
|---|---|---|
| A day at capacity | 34 × 40 L at 45 °C | 1,360 L |
| Heat a kelvin of rise | 1,360 L × 1.163 Wh | about 1.58 kWh |
| From cold water at 8 °C, [assumed] | × 37 K | about 58.5 kWh |
| A year, on use days | 58.5 kWh × 120 | about 7,000 kWh |
| Beech, all by wood | 7,000 ÷ (1,885 × 0.74–0.81) | 4.6–5.0 m³ |
| The paper's tank, 200 L at 60 °C | mixed to 45 °C | about 281 L |

**The hot water.** The papers' 200 L tank holds about a fifth of a full
day at capacity, so it is not a store but a draw-off, reheated from the
buffer through a coil, with the sanitation paper's wood-fired water
heater for the summer, when the boiler is cold. Hot water is held at
55 °C or more where the standard applies (ДБН В.2.5-64:2012, with
Amendment 1), and mixed down at the tap.

**The sun.** Solar collectors give about 350 to 600 kWh a m² a year by
type, flat plate the lower and evacuated tube the higher (Carbon
Trust, for BEIS, 2020; solaranlage-ratgeber, 2026) — about 1.0 to 1.6
kWh a m² a day across the year, more in summer. Two collectors, their
area [set] from the chosen collector, carry a share of the summer's
hot water, [set]. The loop runs on about 40 percent propylene glycol,
good to about −20 °C, checked every two years and replaced every five
to ten (energie-experten, 2020), or as a drainback loop that empties
when the pump stops. A water-filled passive thermosiphon is not used:
it was found safe only in the warmest parts of the United States, and
its freeze valves failed about a quarter of the time in hard frosts
(Burch and Salasovich, NREL, 2005). The solar heat enters the buffer's
lowest zone; the boiler never heats the middle, where it would block
the sun's heat (Solarserver, 2025).

### 3.6 The masonry heater and the cookstove

**The masonry heater.** For the gathering place: at least 800 kg of
mass, the smoke turned at least once through 180°, a gas path at
least twice the firebox's largest dimension, walls averaging no more
than 250 mm, the outside no hotter than 110 °C (Masonry Heater
Association, 1998), fired hot once or twice a day and giving heat for
about 12 to 24 hours (makers' figures, secondary). EN 15250 has been
replaced by EN 16510-2-5:2025, which the maker's declaration names.

**The cookstove.** For the kitchen, if the kitchen module and its
rules allow cooking on wood; its chimney is cleaned monthly under the
fire rules.

**Carbon monoxide.** No Ukrainian rule was found that asks for carbon
monoxide alarms; the design fits them on every floor where a fire
burns and outside every sleeping room, as the US EPA advises (Burn
Wise).

## 7. Operation and maintenance

Responsible: one named adult, trained in firing and in the fire rules,
paid, with a deputy — [from L2]. Firing is adult work only, and no
stove is left unattended.

- **Daily, in the heating season:** the boiler fired hot to fill the
  buffer, and firing ended two hours before sleep; the buffer's top and
  bottom temperatures read; the masonry heater fired once or twice.
- **Before each heating season:** every stove, the boiler, and every
  chimney checked and the result written down; the expansion vessel's
  water level read; the alarms tested.
- **Monthly:** the cookstove's chimney cleaned.
- **Every two or three months:** the other chimneys cleaned, as the
  fire rules set.
- **Spring:** the next winter's wood bought or felled, split, and
  stacked under the roof.
- **Every two years:** the solar glycol checked.

How the programme relates to this work is described in the programme
modules, in standing format.

## 11. Adaptation notes

The design is a seed, not a copy: a different place grows a different
form from it.

- **A colder valley:** a larger buffer for the longer night; more wood,
  bought earlier; the expansion vessel and its pipes insulated.
- **A milder valley:** a smaller buffer; the masonry heater may carry a
  building alone.
- **A smaller group:** one masonry heater and a water heater may carry
  it; no boiler.
- **A larger group:** a boiler room, if the rules ask it above a
  boiler's output.
- **No woodland:** every cubic metre bought; nothing changes but the
  price.
- **A stream:** the micro-hydro module's surplus into the buffer.
- **Buildings that allow no gravity loop:** pumped radiators, with a
  gravity heat leak at the boiler.

## 13. What to check

- **The buildings** — surveyed; each one's heat load at −20 °C, by EN
  12831 or an engineer.
- **The boiler and the buffer** — sized from the loads and the night.
- **The gravity loop** — whether each building allows it.
- **The thermal discharge valve** — whether 1 to 2 bar is enough.
- **The hot water** — hot_water_per_person and the cold water's
  temperature, from a meter and a thermometer.
- **The collectors** — their area and the roof they share with the
  array.
- **The firewood** — a dated local price by the складометр; the wood's
  moisture on delivery.
- **The woodland** — its category and what may be felled, from the
  enquiry.
- **The elders** — the four questions in the pattern line.
- **The enquiries** — clearances, woodshed, boiler room, and the
  two-hour rule.
- **Quotations** — every line in Section 6, and the unpriced ones.
- **The climate zone** — on the official map of ДБН В.2.6-31:2021.
- **The Ukrainian row** — every instrument read and signed.
- **From L2, once built:** build times and out-of-order consequences in
  Section 5; the maintenance record in Section 7; early signs and repair
