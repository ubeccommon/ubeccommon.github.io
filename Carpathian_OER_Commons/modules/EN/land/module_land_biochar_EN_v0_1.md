## 1. Purpose

Char for both nutrient streams, made on the site from its own woody
clearings: a scoop at every use of the bucket toilets, and 10 to 30
percent of every garden compost pile. One kiln, two exits, and nothing
burned that the clearing should have left.

**Six rules.** One kiln, two streams: raw crushed char goes to the
seat and to the garden pile, and each stream charges it; no raw char
goes onto a bed. Dry wood only: feedstock dried below about 20 percent
moisture, because wet wood makes methane. Burn what is cleared, never
what is left: standing and fallen deadwood stays as habitat, as the
programme proposal holds, and the kiln takes only what the clearing
takes. The fire keeps its distances and its days: outside every
distance in Section 4, never unattended, never in a fire-danger period
or a dry spell. Water for the quench from the pond or the rain, never
the spring. The work is paid; toloka is not the name for work the kiln
depends on.

**Connections.** Depends on: the clearing done by the planting module
and the regeneration strand, which sets the feedstock; the land's
category and tenure, from the land register, which decides whether a
felling permit is needed; the written answers on the kiln; the market
garden's pond, or the rainwater module, for the quench; the Phase 1
walk, with the fire distances measured. Feeds: the sanitation toilets
row, with char at the seat, which the terra preta module receives in
every vessel; the compost module, with char for the garden pile.

**Patterns.** Grows from: not yet named. Which trees this valley does
not take, and what it holds about wood from the forest, are for the
elders before the first burn; the literature records forked trees,
trees with "candles", and windthrow left unused for building [for the
elders]. Feeds: not yet named. Resonates with: 104 Site Repair — the
kiln on bare, poorer ground outside every fire distance; 172 Garden
Growing Wild — the kiln burns what the clearing takes and never what
it leaves; 120 Paths and Goals — the kiln on the line cleared material
already travels. In tension with: 181 The Fire — a real fire, kept at
the edge and never at the centre; 7 The Countryside — wood taken only
where the land's tenure and the valley's custom allow; 64 Pools and
Streams — the quench drawn from the pond or the rain, never from the
spring.

## 3. Sizing method

**What you must measure yourself before using this module:**

- **Your char need** — the toilets' char at the seat, from the terra
  preta module, and the garden's char, from the compost module.
- **Your feedstock** — the woody material the clearing actually yields
  in a year, stacked and measured, never estimated from the forest.
- **Your moisture** — the feedstock's, before a burn, by a moisture
  meter or by weighing a sample before and after drying.
- **Your yield** — char out, in litres, against feedstock in, for the
  first burns.
- **Your distances** — as in Section 4, measured with a tape.

**Our example:** char_at_seat, 0.5 L a day; bucket_share, two-thirds;
capacity, 34 people; use_days_per_year, 120; char_density, 186 kg per
m³; char_yield, 15 to 22 percent of dry mass. Values from the site
parameters file, held in the workshop.

### 3.1 The need

| Stream | Calculation | Our example |
|---|---|---|
| The toilets | 34 × two-thirds × 0.5 L × 120 | about 1.4 m³ |
| The garden | 10–30 % of 2.4–14.4 m³ raw | about 0.2–4.3 m³ |
| **A year** | sum | **about 1.6–5.7 m³** |
| By mass | × 186 kg per m³ | about 300–1,060 kg |

The garden's raw piles, 2.4 to 14.4 m³ a year, are the compost
module's kitchen and garden streams before they shrink; its char is
10 to 30 percent by volume, the Ithaka Institute's range (Schmidt, 55
uses of biochar). The paper's 1.5 m³ a year was for every use at 25
people; the toilets' share at 34 with two-thirds of uses in buckets is
about 1.4 m³.

### 3.2 The wood and the burns

| Quantity | Calculation | Our example |
|---|---|---|
| Dry wood, a year | 300–1,060 kg ÷ 15–22 % | about 1.4–7.0 t |
| Char a burn | the kiln's volume, roughly | up to about 1 m³ |
| Burns, a year | 1.6–5.7 m³ ÷ about 1 m³ | about 2–6 |
| Loose brush a burn | ten times the kiln's volume | about 8 m³ |
| Hours a burn | as the feedstock allows | 2 to 8 |

**Yield.** Flame-curtain kilns gave 22 ± 5 percent of dry mass, 20 ± 4
when quenched with water (Cornelissen and colleagues, PLOS ONE, 2016);
Schmidt and Taylor give 15 to 20 percent, and two to eight hours to
make roughly 1 m³ of char in their larger cone (Kon-Tiki, the Biochar
Journal, 2014). A flame-cap kiln uses about ten times its volume in
feedstock (South Umpqua Rural Community Partnership, 2018). Char is
taken at 186 kg per m³, measured from a flame-cap kiln in the field
(Wilson and colleagues, 2024); biochars range from 80 to 320 kg per m³.

**The feedstock decides.** At the upper end the need is about 7 t of
dry wood a year. The programme proposal leaves standing and fallen
deadwood as habitat and clears only what chokes the young growth; if
the clearing yields less than the need, the garden's char is cut
first, and the toilets' char is kept.

### 3.3 The kiln, as designed

| Element | Specification |
|---|---|
| Form | a cone, open at the top, flame-curtain |
| Rim | 1.5 m across |
| Depth | 0.9 m |
| Wall | 63° from the horizontal |
| Base | about 0.58 m across, derived |
| Volume | about 0.82 m³, derived |
| Steel | 3 mm, hot-rolled; about 3.6 m², 84 kg, derived |
| Quench | by bucket from the water store |
| Alternative | the same cone dug in mineral soil |

The rim, depth, and angle are those of Schmidt and Taylor's first
steel cone of about 750 L; the base, volume, area, and mass are derived
from them here, not quoted. The published steel is 2 mm for small cones
and 3 mm for larger ones, a figure to be confirmed against the source.
The design is published by its authors as open source.

Quantities for the example.

| Item | Quantity | Alternatives in the design |

| Quench water store, 1,000 L | 1 | the pond, by pump |
| Woven bags for crushing | [set], from the site | a hammer mill |

**Crushing.** Wet, in closed woven bags, under a roller or a
sledgehammer, to less than 10 mm, then through the sieve (IBI,
Guidelines on Practical Aspects of Biochar Application, 2010; Permachar
Kon-Tiki manual, 2023). Dry char dust can form explosive mixtures with
air in closed spaces, and tightly packed char can heat itself (IBI,
2010): the store keeps char damp, near the 30 percent water the
European Biochar Certificate recommends for storage, and loosely
heaped. A grain hammer mill, listed at 2,799 UAH, is the alternative
for larger batches; wet char clogs it.

**The siting, as designed.** Every distance below is the larger of what
the instruments in Section 10 ask, and every one is measured on the
ground before the first burn; none has been measured yet.

| From the kiln to | At least |
|---|---|
| Any building | 30 m |
| Coniferous forest | 50 m |
| Deciduous forest | 25 m |
| The spring, and never upslope of it | 50 m |
| The stream and the pond, on a slope | 50 m |
| Any place where children are | 30 m |
| A cleared, non-combustible strip round it | 2.5 m wide |

**The layout, as designed.** The kiln on bare, poorer ground, on the
line cleared material already travels; the feedstock stacked to dry
under cover, upwind and outside the cleared strip; the water store
beside the kiln; the char store between the kiln and its two uses.
Drawn as relationships only; the walk sets the rest.

**A burn, as published.** An open stack of dry wood lit at the top; the
next layer added when the top of the last begins to coat with ash; the
flame kept strong so that it burns its own smoke; the char quenched
with water when the last layer has charred, until it floats (Schmidt
and Taylor, 2014; Permachar, 2023). Smoke means too much added at once
(South Umpqua, 2018).

## 7. Operation and maintenance

Responsible: one named adult, paid, with a deputy — [from L2]. Not a
rota. The kiln is never left unattended.

- **Before a burn:** the day checked — no fire-danger period, no dry
  spell of ten days or more, calm or damp weather, no standing order
  against fire; the water store full; the cleared strip clear.
- **During a burn:** the flame kept strong; layers added as the last
  coats with ash; nothing added that is painted, treated, or coated.
- **After a burn:** the char quenched until it floats; drained; taken
  to the store damp; the ash and the ground checked cold before the
  kiln is left.
- **Crushing:** wet, in closed bags, with respirator and goggles; then
  sieved; the fines kept for the seat, the coarse for the garden pile.
- **Seasonal:** feedstock gathered from the clearing and stacked to dry
  under cover a season ahead; the kiln checked for cracks and rust.
- **Annual:** the burn record — date, feedstock, moisture, char out —
  carried into char_yield; the feedstock the clearing gave, against the
  need.

How the programme relates to the kiln is described in the programme
modules, in standing format.

- **Fire Safety Rules of Ukraine, Order No. 1417 of the Ministry of
  Internal Affairs, 30.12.2014.** Point 1.20: a fire site at least 30 m
  from buildings, 50 m from coniferous and 25 m from deciduous forest,
  inside a cleared strip at least 2.5 m wide; open fire never left
  unattended.
- **Fire Safety Rules in the forests of Ukraine, Order No. 278 of
  27.12.2004:** no fire in the forest in the fire-danger period; no
  burning of residues after ten or more days without rain.
- **Law No. 2707-XII of 16.10.1992,** on the protection of atmospheric
  air: Art. 20 bans burning waste in settlements except in special
  installations; Art. 11 asks an emissions permit of business entities
  for stationary sources. No act addresses a small kiln; commercial
  charcoal kilns hold permits, and some have been prosecuted without.
- **Code of Administrative Offences, Art. 77-1:** unauthorised burning
  of vegetation, 3,060 to 6,120 UAH for a citizen, doubled on land of
  the nature-reserve fund. A kiln fed with cut wood is arguably not
  burning off vegetation; that is untested.
- **Wartime.** Regional military administrations have ordered bans in
  forests at high fire danger. The state forestry agency says burning
  dry vegetation in wartime may be treated as sabotage; not verified in
  the Criminal Code. Local orders are asked for, not assumed.
- **Forest Code, No. 3852-XII of 21.01.1994,** Art. 69: any felling on
  forest land, sanitary felling included, needs a felling permit,
  issued free through the online environmental service. Whether an
  owner's collection of brushwood on forest land is free use is not
  verified.
- **Law No. 1436-III of 10.02.2000,** as amended in 2019: no clear
  felling in the Carpathian mountain fir-beech forests.
- **Cabinet Resolution No. 1045 of 01.08.2006:** trees on a homestead
  plot inside a settlement are removed at the owner's decision; the
  point number is not verified.

The written enquiries: to the regional environmental inspectorate and
the regional emergency service, on whether the kiln is a burning of
waste, a burning of vegetation, or a source needing a permit, and on

the wooded ground is forest-fund land; to the regional forestry
authority, on brushwood; to the village council and the regional
military administration, on local rules and orders. No burn is made
before they are answered.

## 11. Adaptation notes

The design is a seed, not a copy: a different place grows a different
form from it.

- **A drier valley:** more fire-danger days and fewer burn days; burn
  in the wet season, store a year's char.
- **A colder valley:** feedstock dries more slowly; stack it two
  seasons ahead.
- **A smaller group:** fewer burns; the same kiln, or a smaller cone of
  2 mm steel.
- **A larger group or a larger garden:** more burns, not a larger
  kiln, until the burn record says otherwise.
- **No steel, or no welder:** the same cone dug in mineral soil.
- **No clearing to speak of:** char bought in, to the European Biochar
  Certificate, and the toilets' share first.

## 13. What to check

- **The written answers** — on the kiln, the wood, the land's category,
  and local orders — before the first burn.
- **The distances** — every row of the siting table, measured with a
  tape on the walk.
- **The feedstock** — what the clearing yields in a year, stacked and
  measured, against 1.4 to 7.0 t of dry wood.
- **char_yield** — char out against wood in, from the first burns.
- **char_density** — a known volume of the site's char, weighed.
- **The steel thickness** — confirmed against Schmidt and Taylor.
- **The char and cover store** — re-estimated for a year's char and
  cover.
- **The garden's char** — from the compost module's piles as measured.
- **The quench water** — litres a burn, from the first burns.
- **The responsible adult** — hours and pay.
- **The Ukrainian row** — every instrument read and signed.
