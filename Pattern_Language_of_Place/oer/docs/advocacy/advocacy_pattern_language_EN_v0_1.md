*Written to be given away. This is the pattern form of a
method set out at length elsewhere: an advocacy reference that
holds the record and the figures, a plain-language guide that
holds the argument, a case history that holds the observation
behind three of the patterns, and a general statement of the
method. Each pattern below is a card in the form this
collection already uses — the tension, what was observed, the
pattern, its connections, its open questions, its status —
with the record standing in for field notes. Read the cards in
order once; after that, enter anywhere. This document was
developed with assistance from Claude (Anthropic PBC). All
strategic decisions and commitments are those of the author.*

* * *

## How to read this

A pattern language is not a list of rules. It is a web of
named relationships across scales, each pattern stating a
tension that recurs, what has been observed about it, and a
resolution that has held — connected to the patterns it
depends on and the ones it supports. Alexander built the form
for buildings; this collection uses it for a place. Here it is
used for advocacy: the craft of trying, on purpose and
together, to change what people think, feel, or do, for
someone or something that cannot do it alone.

The rings are scales, from the smallest outward:

- **Ring 0 — The opening.** One pattern: the question
  everything else answers.
- **Ring 1 — The point.** The single real thing shown, and the
  person who owns the showing.
- **Ring 2 — The work.** What is made, how it returns, what it
  refuses to say.
- **Ring 3 — The carriers.** Where the work is placed, for
  whom, and how it is allowed to change.
- **Ring 4 — The environment.** The information weather the
  work is placed into, and what it must survive.

Status is one of three: *converged* — the pattern appears in
movements that never designed it, on more than one continent;
*measured* — at least one published attribution or natural
experiment stands behind it; *designed* — proposed, not yet
demonstrated by an instance built from it. Where a pattern is
designed only, the card says so.

Every figure quoted on a card is drawn from the advocacy
reference published alongside this document, which cites its
sources by name. Nothing here is new; everything here is
shorter, and shaped to be assembled.

## Ring 0 — The opening

### Pattern 1 — What If

**The tension.** Advocacy usually opens with a claim, and a
claim can be rebutted. The audience's most comfortable
position is futility — nothing can be done, so nothing is
asked of me — and the advocate's is argument — being right is
comfortable; showing and then staying quiet is not. Each
side's comfort protects the other's.

**What was observed.** The lasting successes in the record did
not argue their case. Women refusing sugar in 1791 did not
debate abolition at the table; they put the sugar bowl away. A
German artist setting brass stones into pavements since 1992
explains nothing on the stone. The question those movements
were answering was not "is this right?" but "what if this were
possible?" — and the answer was the thing itself.

**The pattern.** Open with a question, not a claim: *what if
this were possible?* A question cannot be rebutted, only
answered, and the way to answer it is to build the thing and
let time check it. The question is the door out of both
comfort zones at once. Everything that follows in this
language is a way of answering it honestly.

**Connections.** *Supports:* every pattern below — it is the
frame they fill. *Connects to:* Say What Failure Looks Like
(Pattern 7), because a question that cannot be answered "no"
is a claim in disguise.

**Open questions.** Whether the question survives translation
into audiences whose language has no idiomatic *what if*;
whether it can be asked without being heard as a slogan.

**Status.** Converged.

## Ring 1 — The point

### Pattern 2 — One Subject, Revisitable

**The tension.** Advocacy wants scale, and the mind cannot
feel scale. One identifiable person moves us; the numbing
begins as early as the second face; a thousand is a statistic.
But a single subject shown once is a burst, and bursts are
what the field already has too many of.

**What was observed.** The AIDS quilt is fifty thousand
panels, each the size of one grave, each one person. The
stones are one name each. The tree movement in Kenya counted
trees one at a time to fifty-one million. In each case the
unit of encounter stayed single while the count grew — and the
subject was somewhere you could go back to. Compassion
collapse and pseudoinefficacy are named, replicated findings;
the single subject is not a style choice but a cognitive
limit.

**The pattern.** Make the unit of encounter one real subject
in one real place that can be returned to. Aggregate elsewhere
— in the count, the map, the index — never in the encounter. A
subject that cannot be revisited is a picture; a subject that
can is evidence.

**Connections.** *Depends on:* The Person Shown Owns the
Showing (Pattern 3). *Supports:* Show It Again (Pattern 5).
*Connects to:* Empty Region Held Empty (Pattern 4).

**Open questions.** At what count does the index itself become
the spectacle, and the single subject a token?

**Status.** Converged; the cognitive limit measured.

* * *

### Pattern 3 — The Person Shown Owns the Showing

**The tension.** Advocacy needs the story, and the story
belongs to someone. The field's default is extraction — take
the testimony, carry it to the audience, add a consent form
afterward. The person is material. Everything downstream
inherits that.

**What was observed.** The strongest membership movements in
the record were run by the people shown: the mothers in Buenos
Aires were the movement; the quilt panels were sewn by the
dead person's own people; each stone is made only after the
family is asked; #MeToo was testimony at scale with zero
extraction because every post was the poster's own story,
under their own name, at their own risk. Where the person
shown owned the showing, the campaign outlasted its founders.
Where they were material — the viral video of 2012, the
helpless continent of 1985 — the campaign left the wrong
picture behind.

**The pattern.** Consent is architecture, not compliance. The
person shown decides how far the showing travels and how long
it stays, before anything goes out; belonging attaches to
their contribution; withdrawal stays possible along the whole
trace. They are a member of the thing, never its content.

**Connections.** *Depends on:* Built to Survive Hostile Re-use
(Pattern 14) — consent for a campaign is a different consent
from consent for a quiet record. *Supports:* Pattern 2; The
Unit You Share (Pattern 8). *Connects to:* The Swing Pushes
Back (Pattern 12).

**Open questions.** How consent scales without compressing —
growth is bounded by real conversations, and no known shortcut
leaves the pattern intact.

**Status.** Converged.

* * *

### Pattern 4 — Empty Region Held Empty

**The tension.** A half-empty map, a half-told story, a region
with no point in it — every instinct says fill it: a
composite, a placeholder, a representative case. The audience
will not know. The hostile reader will.

**What was observed.** Every fabrication in the record was
found, and the finding cost more than the gap would have. The
organisation behind the 2012 video closed. Programmes that
could not fail visibly — the anti-drug curriculum, the
scare-them-straight prison visits — survived their own null
results for years, then were found to have done harm. Trust,
once read as manufactured, does not return.

**The pattern.** No fabricated subjects, no composites, no
placeholder presented as real. Scaffolding is always flagged
as scaffolding. An empty region is the correct answer until
real, consented material exists. The gap is the evidence of
the standard.

**Connections.** *Depends on:* Pattern 3. *Supports:* Say What
Failure Looks Like (Pattern 7); Time Against Volume (Pattern
13), since only what is verifiable outlasts volume. *Connects
to:* the sincerity condition in the case history — the faked
stimulus gets no response.

**Open questions.** How to show that a region is empty on
purpose, so the emptiness reads as discipline and not as
absence.

**Status.** Designed as a rule; converged as a practice — the
stones and the quilt never faked a name.

## Ring 2 — The work

### Pattern 5 — Show It Again

**The tension.** The feed rewards the burst and forgets in
days. A campaign built for one exposure gets one exposure. But
the same thing shown twice looks like repetition, and
repetition of an argument fatigues.

**What was observed.** The successes that lasted were decades
long — the mothers every Thursday from 1977, the stones from
1992, the trees from 1977. The same place seen again, further
grown, was the argument; nothing was said twice, but something
was shown twice. Psychology supplies the engine: familiarity
breeds liking with no argument occurring; a real, particular
person seen repeatedly at a distance works on the mind like a
neighbour; spaced exposure outlasts massed. The measured
counter-case is the platform-era spike: support at 67 per cent
in June 2020, 51 by 2023.

**The pattern.** Publish only what can be revisited, and put
the return into the form itself — a schedule the audience can
fall into step with. The revisit is the argument. Repetition
of a demonstration accumulates; repetition of a claim decays.

**Connections.** *Depends on:* Pattern 2. *Supports:* Time
Against Volume (Pattern 13) — duration is the one thing a
firehose cannot counterfeit. *Connects to:* Choose Your Swing
(Pattern 10), which sets the rhythm's reach.

**Open questions.** What the minimum honest interval is before
a return reads as growth rather than as the same picture
again.

**Status.** Converged; the mechanisms measured.

* * *

### Pattern 6 — Doors, Not a Hand on the Back

**The tension.** The advocate knows what the audience should
conclude, and wants to say so. But a message experienced as
pressure produces push-back, up to the boomerang; an authority
instructing teenagers not to take drugs is a machine for
producing exactly the opposite.

**What was observed.** The best-measured success in advocacy —
the teen anti-smoking campaign that recouped its own cost —
never told anyone to obey; it exposed the industry and let the
audience finish the meaning as rebellion. The stones do not
explain. The tree has no caption. In deep canvassing, the
strongest durable attitude shift in the literature, the
listener does most of the talking. Attitudes change most when
people articulate the case in their own words.

**The pattern.** No narrator, no ask, no caption drawing the
conclusion. The audience completes the meaning or nothing
happens. On-ramps exist — follow, twin, fund, visit — but
every on-ramp is a door, never a hand on the back. The only
conversion that counts is the step the viewer takes as their
own.

**Connections.** *Depends on:* Pattern 1 — a question leaves
the answer to the audience. *Supports:* The Unit You Share
(Pattern 8). *Connects to:* The Trimtab (Pattern 9) —
placement, not push.

**Open questions.** Whether a form with no narrator can hold
in a medium that inserts one — a platform's own captioning, a
curator's framing.

**Status.** Measured.

* * *

### Pattern 7 — Say What Failure Looks Like

**The tension.** Almost no campaign states in advance what its
own failure would look like — so budgets outlive evidence, and
the programme that cannot fail visibly lasts longest.
Falsifiability feels like weakness in a room that wants
confidence.

**What was observed.** The anti-drug media campaign spent over
$1.4 billion and was found not effective, with possible
delayed adverse effects; nothing in its design could be seen
to fail. The one literature that published its own failure —
deep canvassing, a 2014 study retracted, a 2016 replication
that held — came out stronger. Esperanto ran for more than a
century on duration, membership, and demonstration and never
tipped, because nobody named what actually stopped people.

**The pattern.** State the predicted result before the first
input, including the expected near-null after a single
exposure. Run a hostile-read round that can remove whole
classes of content before they are built. Name the failure
conditions, and the audience's blocking belief — diagnosed,
not assumed. Publish the nulls. A method that cannot say what
would disprove it is a belief.

**Connections.** *Depends on:* Pattern 4. *Supports:* Choose
Your Swing (Pattern 10), which sets what is measured.
*Connects to:* Pattern 1 — the question's honest "no."

**Open questions.** What instrument measures a crack rather
than a conversion; how a small project reads public polling as
terrain without commissioning any.

**Status.** Designed — the converged cases never carried it by
intent; it is what deliberate design adds.

* * *

### Pattern 8 — The Unit You Share

**The tension.** The platforms amplify. Everyone wants the
amplification. But the same amplifier that made the 2012 video
the most viral of its era gave it a two-year half-life and a
sequel nobody watched.

**What was observed.** Hand the platform a message and you get
a spike and a fade. Hand it an act people perform themselves —
my story under my name, my bucket of ice water, my abstained
sugar — and each share is itself a small demonstration, and
the effect accumulates: #MeToo, the ice-bucket summer, the
sugar boycott. One photograph in 2015 measured both halves at
once: daily donations rose a hundredfold and faded almost as
fast, while monthly sign-ups rose tenfold and almost none
cancelled. The people the burst turned into members stayed.

**The pattern.** A message decays; a membership act
accumulates. Design the shareable unit as an act the sharer
performs, not a message they forward. The unit you share
decides everything the platform will do with it.

**Connections.** *Depends on:* Pattern 3 — the act belongs to
the person who performs it. *Supports:* Show It Again (Pattern
5). *Connects to:* Time Against Volume (Pattern 13).

**Open questions.** How an act stays an act when the platform
converts it to a template — the point at which a gesture
becomes a share.

**Status.** Measured.

## Ring 3 — The carriers

### Pattern 9 — The Trimtab

**The tension.** The audience is heavy and the advocate is
small. Pushing directly costs more than any small project has,
and the record says bought reach for a message is the most
expensive way to produce nothing.

**What was observed.** On a great ship the rudder is too heavy
to push directly, so a small tab on the rudder's edge turns
the rudder, and the rudder turns the ship. A fund holding two
hundredths of a per cent of the largest oil company won three
board seats by persuading the few giant index holders whose
votes turn it — not by fighting the ship. The smiling-sun logo
of a fifty-year movement was never bought; it travelled
through carrier networks. Placement before volume; carriers
before crowds.

**The pattern.** Do not push the audience. Place one small,
true, well-made thing where the medium's own flow is already
moving — the twinning, the aid group, the local newsletter,
the teacher — and let the flow do the work. The trimtab has a
second meaning, from aircraft, and it may be the truer one:
fitted so the pilot is not worn out holding a course.

**Connections.** *Depends on:* Pattern 6. *Supports:* Choose
Your Swing (Pattern 10); One Drop Point (Pattern 11).
*Connects to:* the receiver-side energy in the case history —
the response is built from what the audience already holds.

**Open questions.** Whether a trimtab can be identified in
advance or only in hindsight; how many carriers a single point
can hold before it is no longer a point.

**Status.** Converged; the financial case measured.

* * *

### Pattern 10 — Choose Your Swing

**The tension.** Every funder's criteria ask for reach, and
every campaign claims both reach and depth. But depth of
response and breadth of audience trade off — for oscillators,
by theorem; for anything human, as a design heuristic that has
never been repealed.

**What was observed.** Someone pushing a child on a swing has
the whole method in their hands: small force, matched to a
rhythm already there, at the right moment, many times. Push
out of time and the swing dies; push harder out of time and it
still dies. One swing can be pushed deep; a playground can be
pushed shallow; not both. The record's deepest successes tuned
sharply to a few whose own audiences then moved with them.

**The pattern.** Choose selectivity, and say which. A narrow,
well-defined audience with a deep, accumulating response is a
legitimate and usually superior choice to broad reach with
fast decay. Name the choice per campaign, because it also
chooses what is measured: a campaign tuned to a narrow band
will not move a national poll, and expecting it to is a
category error. Most criteria offer targeted influence as an
alternative to mass reach; take it, out loud.

**Connections.** *Depends on:* Pattern 9. *Supports:* Say What
Failure Looks Like (Pattern 7) — the instrument follows the
choice. *Connects to:* One Drop Point (Pattern 11).

**Open questions.** How a narrow-band success is later widened
without losing the depth that made it — whether a second swing
can be pushed from the first.

**Status.** Designed; the trade-off observed, not measured in
advocacy.

* * *

### Pattern 11 — One Drop Point

**The tension.** A good idea wants to be everywhere at once.
But inputs that are out of phase with each other cancel: drop
the same thing into five audiences on five rhythms and you
have spent five inputs to produce nothing anyone can feel.

**What was observed.** Two images hold the decision. The
pebble into the pond: one strike, and the medium's own
coupling carries the wave outward from a single entry. The
small hammer on the mountain: the same spot, returned to, blow
after patient blow. They differ in what follows — propagation
outward, accumulation in place — and both agree that the entry
point is one, and chosen.

**The pattern.** One entry point, one rhythm, proven or
falsified, then the next. Choose it against stated criteria: a
rhythm already running, not still water; a carrier whose own
audience demonstrably follows them; low damping — the moveable
middle, not a band already saturated to futility; a medium
that structurally supports return (a newsletter can; a feed
cannot); and survivable placement — consent, safety, and
capture design travel with the input, and a drop point that
exposes anyone is disqualified whatever its coupling.

**Connections.** *Depends on:* Patterns 9 and 10. *Supports:*
Show It Again (Pattern 5) — the rhythm is set here. *Connects
to:* Built to Survive Hostile Re-use (Pattern 14).

**Open questions.** Whether the first drop point is ever
really a choice — the room in which the work is first shown is
the default unless deliberately declined — so the real
decision is the second.

**Status.** Designed.

* * *

### Pattern 12 — The Swing Pushes Back

**The tension.** Physics leaves the driver unmoved. Advocacy
borrows the physics and forgets that every human account of
the same phenomenon — two millennia apart — insists that both
parties change, or it was not response. A campaign that cannot
be altered by its audience is broadcasting with better
manners.

**What was observed.** The lived concepts of mutual work —
toloka, dugnad, meitheal, imece, harambee, bayanihan — all put
belonging after the contribution: the log you carried, not the
speech you made. The Kenyan trees fed the women who planted
them and changed the movement's own shape as they did. Working
with, not for, is not a courtesy; it is the two-way condition.

**The pattern.** What comes back from the audience must be
able to alter the work — its form, its subjects, its schedule.
Build the return path into the design, and let the audience's
own step — join, twin, fund, visit — be the membership, with
the campaign being what those steps add up to.

**Connections.** *Depends on:* Pattern 3. *Supports:* Say What
Failure Looks Like (Pattern 7) — feedback is also evidence.
*Connects to:* the mutual-transformation finding in the case
history.

**Open questions.** How much the work may change before it is
no longer the work — the boundary between responsiveness and
drift.

**Status.** Converged as practice; designed as rule.

## Ring 4 — The environment

### Pattern 13 — Time Against Volume

**The tension.** The information environment is shaped by an
adversarial method — high volume, many channels, no commitment
to truth or consistency — whose product is not belief but
exhaustion: the feeling that nothing can be known and nothing
done. Matching it with volume loses by construction.

**What was observed.** Read as design rather than content, the
firehose is a damping strategy: incoherent volume raises
everyone's friction until no signal can accumulate. It has
learned to buy rather than build — a 2024 indictment showed
one commentator on four hundred thousand dollars a month — and
synthetic generation is collapsing the cost of fabrication
toward zero. But it has one structural weakness: it can fake
any single picture and any single story, and it cannot fake
the same real place, revisited, over years. A lie must be
maintained; a garden only watered. Four hundred thousand a
month for one rented voice; one hundred and twenty euros for
one permanent stone.

**The pattern.** Against a damping strategy, coherence over
time is the one thing that still accumulates. Build a small
set of things that are always checkable — verified,
provenanced, returned to — and let time do what volume cannot.
Time against volume is the asymmetric answer, and it costs
almost nothing.

**Connections.** *Depends on:* Patterns 4 and 5. *Supports:*
the whole language — it is the reason the language exists.
*Connects to:* Pattern 8.

**Open questions.** Damping may win; against sufficiently
manufactured futility, coherence over time is the best
available answer and still may not accumulate. No pattern here
can promise otherwise.

**Status.** Measured in its adversary; designed in its answer.

* * *

### Pattern 14 — Built to Survive Hostile Re-use

**The tension.** Everything published can be turned. The same
craft that carries a memorial has carried tyrannies; the same
model that finds a community's frequency lets a wrecker attack
it through that frequency with small force — a precise
description of targeted disinformation. Advocacy in a hostile
environment, about real people in real places, has costs the
people carry and the campaign does not.

**What was observed.** The 1985 sympathy campaign built the
wrong permanent picture of a continent, still contested forty
years on. The 2012 video's extraction was exactly what its
subjects' backlash was about. A tapestry at the United Nations
was covered over during a war briefing because everyone in the
building knew what it argued. Craft guarantees nothing; only
design against capture does.

**The pattern.** Build every output to survive hostile re-use,
and when in doubt, leave it unpublished. Exclude by design the
classes of subject whose exposure cannot be made safe — in
wartime: children, precise places, schedules tied to named
people; no gate opens this. Hand no adversary a frequency map
of the community shown — what moves them, when, through whom.
Sometimes declining synchrony is the design: soldiers break
step on a bridge.

**Connections.** *Depends on:* Pattern 3 — consent that names
the reach and the permanence is the first capture defence.
*Supports:* Patterns 4 and 11. *Connects to:* every pattern in
Ring 1.

**Open questions.** Who decides "in doubt" when the person
shown wants exposure the designer judges unsafe — the pattern
says the designer declines, and says why.

**Status.** Designed; its failures converged.

## Assembling your own

The cards are meant to be laid out, not read through. A way to
begin:

1. Write Pattern 1 as a sentence about your own situation:
   *what if ___ were possible?*
2. Choose Ring 1 first — one real subject, one owner. If you
   cannot name both, stop; the language has nothing to say
   yet.
3. Take the four Ring 2 cards as four promises, and test them
   together: any three without the fourth collapse into
   something the world already has — duration without
   ownership is surveillance; ownership without showing is a
   newsletter; showing without a failure test is faith.
4. Choose your swing and your drop point (Ring 3) before your
   first input, and write the predicted response and the
   failure condition down with them.
5. Read the result against Ring 4 as a hostile reader would,
   before one does.

Then the five questions the whole language reduces to: Is
there a real thing I can come back to? Does the person shown
own it, or am I using them? Can it stand without a narrator?
What would tell me it isn't working? What is the one belief
actually stopping my audience — named, not guessed?
