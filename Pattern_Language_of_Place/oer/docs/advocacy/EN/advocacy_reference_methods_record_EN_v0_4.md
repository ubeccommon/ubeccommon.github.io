*Written to be given away. This document is a reference for
people about to make advocacy: it defines advocacy, surveys
the methods the field has used, records what succeeded and
what failed and at what cost, and closes with an alternative —
one author's proposed method, a trimtab method, offered to be
used, changed, or discarded. The licence permits all of that.
Figures are cited to their sources in §10. This document was
developed with assistance from Claude (Anthropic PBC). All
strategic decisions and commitments are those of the author.*

* * *

## Introduction

This reference exists so that a room full of people about to
invent advocacy campaigns can stand on the field's whole
record instead of its habits. It defines the craft (§1), lays
out the methods in use (§2), and walks the record of what
succeeded and what failed, with the costs attached (§3–§5) —
because the costs are the least-told part of the story and the
most instructive. It then opens the psychology beneath the
record — the named, studied mechanisms of why persuasion fails
and demonstration works — and examines the two great carriers,
art and marketing. It names the adversary's method rather than
politely omitting it (§6), and then offers one alternative
built from what the record teaches (§7) — its four
commitments, the design model beneath them, three design
rules, and the instruments that make its falsifiability real —
with its limits stated (§8) and an invitation attached (§9).
Every figure traces to a named source (§10).

How to use it: read your own idea against §5's pattern and
§7's five-part test the way a hostile reader would, before one
does. The document is a whetstone, not a doctrine — §9 says
what to do with it.

* * *

## Summary

Advocacy has three modes: speaking for, speaking to, and
showing. The record is unsentimental about which works. The
great failures — a billion-dollar campaign that may have
backfired, the most viral video of its era achieving nothing —
share four properties: episodic, extractive, persuasive,
unfalsifiable. The lasting successes — decades-long, cheap per
point, owned by the people shown — are durational, membered,
demonstrative, and measured. The platform era amplified this
split: the feed rewards the burst, and movements grew faster,
larger, and shallower. The dividing line is not the budget but
the unit shared: a message decays; a membership act
accumulates.

The adversary's method, the firehose, manufactures futility
rather than belief — and its structural weakness is that it
can fake any single story but cannot fake the same verifiable
place revisited over years. From this the alternative in §7
follows: duration as evidence, membership not extraction,
demonstration not persuasion, falsifiability in the room —
four commitments, load- bearing together, with a five-part
test for building an instance of your own. The method is slow,
bounded by consent, and wrong for crisis response; those
limits are accepted and stated. The psychology beneath all of
this is named and studied — reactance, dissonance, contact,
learned helplessness — and the middle of the document walks
it, along with the two great carriers: art is demonstration's
native language, marketing its craft of legibility — both
carriers, neither a method, and the strongest creative
advocacy is art the audience makes itself.

Beneath the method sits a design model, borrowed as structure
from the physics of driven oscillation and as ethic from a
convergence of older traditions, and bounded explicitly by
what does not transfer: the advocate's task is tuning, not
transmission. Find the audience's frequency rather than
broadcasting your own; place small inputs in phase; repeat
coherently; choose depth or breadth and say which; expect to
be changed. The model supplies what the four commitments left
implicit — three design rules (selectivity named, one drop
point in sequence, feedback that can alter the work) and a
measurement architecture: instruments matched to the chosen
selectivity, a baseline before the first input, benchmark
curves taken from the record rather than invented, nulls
published. It reads the firehose as a damping strategy,
against which volume is the wrong physics and coherence over
time the one thing that still accumulates. The whole document
is licensed to be taken, changed, and outgrown.

## 1. What advocacy is

From the Latin *advocare* — to call to one's aid. An advocate
is one who speaks for another, or for a cause, toward those
with the power to act.

A working definition: **advocacy is the organised attempt to
change what people believe, feel, or do about something, on
behalf of someone or something that cannot make that change
alone.**

Three modes hide inside the definition, and most methods mix
them without noticing:

- **Speaking for** — representation. Giving voice to those
  unheard: testimony, witness, legal advocacy.
- **Speaking to** — persuasion. Arguments, appeals, framing,
  campaigns aimed at an audience's beliefs.
- **Showing** — demonstration. Building or revealing the thing
  itself and letting the audience conclude.

Adjacent forms, worth separating: **propaganda** disregards
truth in service of the sponsor; **marketing** serves a
transaction; **activism** is the broader category of acting
for change, of which advocacy is the communicative part. The
boundary that matters most in wartime: advocacy that
disregards truth has become propaganda, whatever it calls
itself.

The practice is old. The first recorded collective action for
redress is the Deir el-Medina strike of about 1157 BC — tomb
artisans sitting at the temples until overdue grain rations
were paid, and winning. Rome's secessio plebis of 494 BC won
the tribunate by mass withdrawal from the city — refusal of
participation as leverage. Advocacy is not a modern invention;
only its industrialisation is.

## 2. The methods — a short taxonomy

The field's main approaches, each with its core mechanism:

- **Rebuttal and fact-checking.** Correct the false claim.
  Mechanism: accuracy. Known weakness: repeating a claim to
  refute it strengthens its familiarity.
- **Framing and narrative change.** Change the frame, not the
  facts (Lakoff; FrameWorks). Mechanism: repetition installs
  frames. Weakness: decade-scale, infrastructure- heavy.
- **Deep canvassing.** One non-judgmental conversation in
  which the listener talks and exchanges a personal story
  (Broockman and Kalla). The strongest measured durable
  attitude shift in the literature. Weakness: one-to-one
  economics.
- **Solutions journalism.** Report responses, not only
  problems. Measured effect: higher reader efficacy, less news
  avoidance.
- **Social norms.** Show that the desired behaviour is common
  (Cialdini). Weakness: showing a bad behaviour as common
  normalises it — several campaigns backfired here.
- **Moral reframing.** Argue in the audience's own moral
  vocabulary, not the speaker's (Feinberg and Willer).
- **Hope-based communications.** Lead with the vision, not the
  violation — the human-rights field's own correction after
  outrage stopped working.
- **Entertainment-education.** Serialized fiction carrying
  attitude change (radio dramas; telenovelas). Works — and is
  fiction, with everything that costs in trust.
- **Testimony and witness.** Documented suffering carried to
  those who can act (Amnesty model). Risk: extraction — the
  subject becomes material.
- **Counter-mapping and crisis-mapping.** Change what the map
  shows (Ushahidi lineage); or change the map's unit entirely.
- **Twinning and sponsorship.** Durable relationships as the
  medium: sister cities, community refugee sponsorship —
  participation itself converts attitudes over years.
- **Strategic litigation.** Change the rule rather than the
  attitude: the NAACP's twenty-year path to Brown v. Board;
  Urgenda (2019), where the Dutch Supreme Court ordered the
  state to cut emissions 25%. Durational, low-membered,
  decisive where a rights framework exists to invoke.
- **Insider advocacy — lobbying.** The field's biggest spend:
  access, drafting, relationships (US federal lobbying alone
  exceeds $4 billion a year). Effective, opaque, access-priced
  — the least membered method in existence, and the one money
  likes best.
- **Community organizing.** Build power through relational
  infrastructure and win by negotiation (Alinsky; the IAF
  tradition): the outcome is a seat at the table, not an
  attitude change.
- **Economic pressure — boycott, divestment, sanctions.** Make
  complicity costly and refusal cheap; participation itself is
  the message. Slow, coercive, membered at mass scale;
  decisive mainly when it reaches finance rather than trade.
  Refusal alone is half a method — the strongest campaigns
  welded it to a constructive programme: Gandhi's khadi was a
  no to Manchester and a visible yes to an existing Indian
  capacity, in one garment. The full instrument set has four
  verbs: refusal (boycott), exit (divestment), voice
  (shareholder activism), and coordinated entry (the buycott —
  weaponised at GameStop, §4).
- **Art and cultural work.** Image, theatre, music, and made
  object as the carrier: from the Brookes diagram to the AIDS
  quilt. Art is demonstration's native language — it shows,
  and the viewer finishes — and it is unit-agnostic: the same
  craft has served memorials and tyrannies. See the art and
  marketing section.
- **Demonstration and prefigurative politics.** Build the
  alternative and let it argue: Fuller — a new model that
  makes the existing model obsolete.
- **Platform and influencer advocacy.** Renting industrialised
  attention and parasocial trust in the creator economy. Not a
  method but an amplifier with an episodic bias: the algorithm
  rewards the burst. It succeeds when the shared unit is a
  membership act performed by the sharer, and decays when the
  unit is a message (§5).
- **Social marketing and brand activism.** The advertising
  discipline applied to causes (Kotler and Zaltman, 1971):
  audience research, message testing, distinctive assets,
  consistency — and, lately, brands taking public stances. The
  techniques transfer; the transaction logic does not. See the
  art and marketing section.
- **Satire and culture jamming.** Adversarial humour; can
  topple a regime (Otpor) and cannot be gentle.
- **The firehose.** The adversary's method — see §6. Named
  here because a taxonomy that omits it explains nothing about
  the present information environment.

## 3. The record — successes, with costs

**British abolition (1787–1833).** The founding modern
campaign — twelve men in a London print shop who invented most
of the toolkit within five years: Clarkson's
evidence-gathering from sailors and ships' doctors (the first
investigative advocacy), the Brookes slave-ship diagram
(demonstration by image, no caption needed), Wedgwood's
kneeling-figure medallion (membership made wearable),
Equiano's first-person testimony, mass petitions at
unprecedented scale, and the 1791–92 sugar boycott — some
300,000 households abstaining, run substantially by women, for
whom the sugar bowl was the one political instrument
available. Twenty years to abolish the trade, forty-six to end
slavery in the empire — and the record's most sobering cost
line: emancipation was purchased with £20 million in
compensation paid to the slaveholders, financed by a loan not
fully retired until 2015. Even the founding success paid its
victory in the currency of the powerful.

**MADD (US, 1980– ).** Survivor-led norms change plus
legislation; drunk-driving deaths roughly halved from about
22,000 in 1980 to about 10,500 by 2018. Grassroots chapter
economics — likely the cheapest large-scale attitude reversal
on record. The shown people owned the showing. And an honest
coda: the halving has partly reversed — about 13,500
alcohol-involved deaths in 2022, a third above 2019, as
pandemic-era enforcement and cultural pressure slackened.
Norms are maintained, not achieved; duration is a practice,
not a trophy.

**The truth campaign (US, 2000– ).** The best-measured success
in advocacy. About $324 million spent 2000–2002; a published
estimate attributes 22% of the 1999–2002 decline in youth
smoking directly to it; roughly $1.9 billion in medical costs
averted — the campaign recouped its cost, at about $544 per
averted adolescent smoker. Register: it never told teens to
obey; it exposed industry manipulation and let teens complete
the meaning as rebellion.

**Marriage equality (US, 1990s–2015).** Decades and hundreds
of millions across the movement. Rights-arguments lost ballot
measures for years; attitudes moved when the abstract became a
visible neighbour — demonstration succeeding where persuasion
failed on the same question.

**Jubilee 2000 (global).** Over $100 billion of poor-country
debt cancelled, from a church-basement coalition: one concrete
ask, one date, carried by existing congregational networks
rather than bought reach.

**ALS Ice Bucket Challenge (2014).** $115 million in eight
weeks at near-zero campaign cost; $1 million of it funded the
gene-sequencing project that identified NEK1. Viral success
came here from the audience performing the act themselves —
the share was the act.

**#MeToo (2017– ).** The clearest social-media success, and
its anatomy explains it: every post was the poster's own
story, self-published, under their own name and at their own
risk — testimony at scale with zero extraction. The shown
people were the showers. It produced real institutional
outcomes: prosecutions, NDA reform, state laws. The hashtag
was a membership act, not a message.

**Stolpersteine (Germany, 1992– ).** The 100,000th stone laid
in May 2023; stones across dozens of European countries; each
financed by sponsorship at 120 euros, each biography
researched and each family asked for consent before a stone is
made. A name, dates, a place — "Here lived" — no narrator. The
largest decentralised memorial in the world, at the price of a
dinner per point. Durational, membered, demonstrative,
verified — three decades before this document names those
words.

**The AIDS Memorial Quilt (US, 1987– ).** Begun from placards
taped to a federal building; 1,920 panels on the National Mall
in 1987, about 50,000 today carrying more than 110,000 names —
54 tons, the largest community art work in the world,
nominated for a Nobel Peace Prize. Each panel is three by six
feet, the size of a grave, and is made by the dead person's
own people — for many, the only funeral that stigma allowed.
The 1996 full display drew 1.2 million visitors. Read against
§7: durational, membered to the stitch, demonstrative — a name
and a life, no caption arguing anything — and verified,
because love does not fabricate its dead. Art as advocacy at
its fullest: the memorial is the campaign, and the making of
it was the membership.

**Green Belt Movement (Kenya, 1977– ).** From seven seedlings
in 1977 to more than 51 million trees and 30,000 women trained
in income-generating trades; survived a decade of state
repression; Nobel Peace Prize 2004. The advocacy was the
growing: each tree simultaneously proof, income, soil, and
membership. Among the lowest cost-per-outcome ratios in the
field's history.

**Treatment Action Campaign (South Africa, 1998– ).** Forced a
denialist government to provide HIV treatment. Members wore
their stigmatised status openly — the shown people were the
movement — combined with litigation.

**Anti-apartheid boycott and divestment (1959–1991).** Among
the largest membered campaigns in history by participation:
three decades of consumer refusal, the Barclays withdrawal
after its share of new student accounts collapsed, and the
sports and cultural boycott — which cost almost nothing and
cut deepest, telling white South Africa in its own most-valued
register that it had become a pariah. Recorded with the
multi-causal caveat: internal mass resistance and the 1985
refusal of banks to roll over South Africa's loans outweighed
three decades of trade measures. The boycott set the climate;
the townships and finance forced the end.

**Engine No. 1 (US, 2021).** The financial trimtab: a fund
holding about 0.02% of ExxonMobil won three board seats with a
proxy campaign reported at roughly $12.5 million — not by
fighting the ship but by persuading the few giant index
holders whose votes turn it. Among the smallest
lever-to-outcome ratios in the modern record.

**Samsø (Denmark, 1997– ).** An island that made itself
renewable-energy self-sufficient in a decade, islanders owning
turbine shares. It never argued the transition was possible;
it became the place you point to.

**German anti-nuclear movement (1970s–2023).** Fifty years
from village occupation to legal phaseout; the smiling-sun
logo was Danish (OOA, 1975) and spread through carrier
networks, never bought.

**Gandhi's salt march (1930); Madres de Plaza de Mayo
(Argentina, 1977– ); Chipko (India, 1970s).** The archetypes:
demonstrating an existing capacity rather than petitioning
against a law; walking the same circle weekly under a
dictatorship until the dictatorship ended; bodies around trees
as physical fact. Durational, membered, largely wordless.

**Otpor (Serbia, 1998–2000).** Helped bring down Milošević
with humour, dilemma actions, and easy visible membership —
the strikes and the security services' defection did the final
work. Recorded as a success — and as a mode (adversarial
satire) that does not transfer to every context; what
transfers is the membership mechanics.

**NAFO (2022– ).** Decentralised, self-funded, crowd-organised
support for Ukraine: the fella avatar as a membership token
earned by donating, millions raised, and a real degradation of
Russian official accounts' ability to operate unmocked. A
success in the same adversarial- satirical mode as Otpor, with
the same caveat — the membership mechanics transfer; the
register does not transfer everywhere.

## 4. The record — failures, with costs

**Kony 2012.** The most viral video of its era — over 100
million views in under a week — raised about $30 million;
funds were exhausted within roughly two years, the stated
objective was never achieved, the sequel drew under 1% of the
original audience, and the organisation announced its own
closure. Episodic, extractive (the African backlash was about
exactly this), persuasive, and unfalsifiable until reality
falsified it.

**US National Youth Anti-Drug Media Campaign (1998–2006).**
Over $1.4 billion appropriated. The government's own audit
found credible evidence the campaign was not effective at
reducing youth drug use — and the core evaluation found
possible delayed unfavourable effects: more ad exposure
predicting weaker anti-drug norms and later initiation. Pure
persuasion at maximum budget, with a plausible norms backfire:
saturation taught teens the behaviour was common.

**D.A.R.E. (classic curriculum).** Hundreds of millions per
year across US localities for two decades; meta-analyses
repeatedly found effects near zero. Authority-figure
persuasion — the register the truth campaign succeeded by
inverting. It survived its own null evaluations for years
because nothing in its design could fail visibly.

**Scared Straight.** Systematic reviews found participants
more likely to offend than controls — an iatrogenic program
whose cost-benefit runs steeply negative once induced crime is
counted. Fear-based demonstration aimed at the audience:
demonstration weaponised as persuasion.

**Remain (UK, 2016).** Establishment backing, a government
mailing to every household, and a fear-forward economic case
against a simple affirmative frame. Lost. Threat framing
against a side offering agency and identity is a losing
register.

**Band Aid / Live Aid (1985).** A fundraising success and a
mental-map failure: it deposited a picture of a helpless,
undifferentiated continent that African critics have spent
forty years contesting. The standing warning: a sympathy
campaign built on extracted suffering works short-term and
builds the wrong permanent map — pity hardens into futility.

**Fridays for Future.** Enormous salience gains and weekly
repetition — but persuasive in register, an ask in every
frame, and salience decayed with polarisation. Repetition of
an argument fatigues; repetition of a demonstration
accumulates.

**#BringBackOurGirls (2014).** Global virality reaching the
White House — and most of the Chibok girls remained captive
for years, with dozens unaccounted for a decade later.
Virality is not leverage when the target does not care about
the audience: the 1933 lesson at fibre-optic speed.

**GameStop (2021).** The inverse of a boycott: coordinated
buying as a weapon. A short-seller lost 53% in a month and
later closed; then the platform halted buying mid-surge and
the movement collapsed within two weeks. Three honest readings
at once: a new form (coordinated market entry); membership at
its most extreme (own money, own risk, identity proven by
willingness to lose); and a failed advocacy campaign — motives
commingled with profit handed every hostile reader the
permanent rebuttal, late buyers absorbed the losses, and no
structural reform followed. Its one durable product was
accidental: the halt itself demonstrated, live, whose side the
market's plumbing sits on — a legibility event no argument
could have staged.

## 5. What the record teaches

The failures share four properties: they are **episodic** (a
burst, then nothing), **extractive** (subjects as material),
**persuasive** (the meaning is asserted, not completed by the
audience), and **unfalsifiable** (no stated failure condition,
so budgets outlive evidence). They are also the most expensive
per unit of nothing: $1.4 billion for a possible backfire; $30
million for a viral null.

The successes are **durational** (decades: MADD, marriage
equality, Stolpersteine, Green Belt, the Madres), or
**membered** (the shown people own the showing: MADD, TAC, Ice
Bucket, Jubilee's congregations, Stolperstein sponsors), or
**demonstrative** (truth's exposure, visible households, an
island, a tree), or **measured** (truth's attribution study).
One pattern in the money: with one exception, the deepest
successes needed no media buy at all. The exception maps the
rule — truth bought reach for a demonstration unit and
measured itself, and worked; the billion-dollar nulls bought
reach for a message. The purchase is not the variable. The
unit is.

The platform pattern now has measurement. The 2020 racial
justice wave produced the largest protests in US history;
Pew's series shows support at 67% in June 2020, 55% by
September, and 51% by 2023 — a spike and reversion at
algorithmic speed. The amplifier is real and its bias is
episodic: it delivers salience and cannot deliver duration.
Hand it a message and you get the curve; hand it a membership
act — my story, my drenching, my abstained sugar — and each
share is itself a small demonstration performed by the sharer,
and the effect accumulates. The platform amplifies whatever
unit it is given. The unit decides everything.

The field's largest dataset agrees from another angle. Across
323 maximalist campaigns from 1900 to 2006, nonviolent
campaigns succeeded 53% of the time against 26% for violent
ones, and none failed after sustained active participation of
3.5% of the population (Chenoweth and Stephan). The sobering
update: since 2010 the success rate of nonviolent campaigns
has fallen below 34%, with participation depth declining
decade on decade — the platform era made movements faster,
larger, and shallower (Tufekci). Mass membership is the
variable; the feed cannot supply it.

One caution against overclaiming: successes attract post-hoc
method attribution. MADD's halving also owes to safer cars,
seatbelt laws, demographics; the clean attribution evidence is
the truth campaign's quasi-experimental 22%. Cite that one as
proof; let the others stand as pattern.

And one convergence finding, stronger than any single case:
the durational-membered-demonstrative combination emerged
independently on at least four continents — Germany, Kenya,
Argentina, South Africa, and the quilt in the United States —
almost always in movements too poor to buy reach. That is
convergent evolution. It suggests the combination is not a
style but what advocacy becomes when it cannot afford to fail
expensively.

One honest counter-case keeps the convergence claim from
overreaching. Esperanto: more than a century durational,
deeply membered, itself a working demonstration of its own
claim — and it never tipped. Three commitments without a named
blocking belief and an on-ramp the audience can actually
afford (adoption cost, network effects) still lose. The
combination recurs among winners; it does not guarantee
winning — which is why the method in §7 carries four
commitments, not three.

## The psychology beneath the record

Sections 3–5 show that the pattern holds. Psychology explains
why. Nearly every finding above sits on a named,
experimentally studied mechanism — which converts the four
commitments in §7 from design taste into mechanism-backed
choices, and tells a campaign designer not just what worked,
but what the mind was doing while it worked.

**Why persuasion fails.** Reactance (Brehm, 1966): people
defend their freedom to conclude for themselves, and a message
experienced as pressure produces push-back — up to the
boomerang, where the audience moves the other way. D.A.R.E.
was a reactance machine: authority instructing adolescents.
The elaboration likelihood model (Petty and Cacioppo): change
is durable only via the central route — the person generating
the reasoning themselves — while received messages ride the
peripheral route and fade with the campaign. Self-persuasion
(Janis and King; Aronson): attitudes shift most when people
articulate the case in their own words — which is what the
listener does in deep canvassing, and what a viewer does in
front of an uncaptioned demonstration. Motivated reasoning and
identity-protective cognition (Kunda; Kahan): facts that
threaten group identity are filtered before they can persuade;
the much-cited backfire effect turns out to be rare (Wood and
Porter), but corrections still underperform — the frame
survives the fact-check. Persuasion is not merely weak; past a
threshold of felt pressure it is counterproductive.

**Why demonstration works.** Mere exposure (Zajonc): repeated
unforced encounter breeds familiarity and liking with no
argument occurring — duration's quiet engine. Contact theory
(Allport; Pettigrew and Tropp's meta-analysis across hundreds
of studies): sustained contact reduces prejudice, and
parasocial contact — mediated exposure to a real, particular
person — carries a measurable share of the effect. This is why
marriage equality moved when the abstract became a neighbour,
and it is the mechanism of a revisited point: manufactured
neighbourship at distance. Narrative transportation (Green and
Brock): absorption in a story lowers counterarguing — the mind
stops debating and starts inhabiting; a form with no narrator
maximises this, because there is no claim to argue with. And
the spacing effect, among the oldest findings in memory
research: distributed exposure outlasts massed exposure. The
revisit pulse is spaced repetition; the viral burst is massed
practice — vivid, and gone.

**Why membership works.** Attitudes follow behaviour more
reliably than behaviour follows attitudes. Cognitive
dissonance (Festinger; the 1959 experiment in which one dollar
changed minds and twenty dollars did not): a freely chosen act
restructures belief to match it, and the smaller the external
justification, the deeper the shift. Foot-in-the-door
(Freedman and Fraser): one small performed act — a stone
sponsored, a tree planted, a bucket poured — makes the next
act consistent with a new self-image. Effort and cost bind
identity: the costliest signals — own money at risk, own name
on a story — fuse members to movements; the diamond hand and
the fella are costly-signalling badges. The token-support
caveat (Kristofferson): cheap public gestures predict nothing
further unless the act links to identity — which is precisely
the difference between a share and a membership act, measured.

**Why futility is the blocking state.** The fear-appeal
meta-analyses (Witte and Allen): fear moves people only when
paired with believable efficacy — threat without a doable
response produces denial, avoidance, and message rejection.
That is Remain, Scared Straight, and the pity-to-futility
hardening after Band Aid, in one model. Learned helplessness
(Seligman and Maier): uncontrollability, demonstrated
repeatedly, teaches a passivity that generalises beyond its
source — and its documented treatment is demonstrated
contingency, the experience that action produces effect. A
growing thing, revisited, is demonstrated contingency in
public. Compassion collapses at scale (Slovic; Västfjäll): one
identifiable person moves us, and the numbing begins as early
as the second — the single subject as the unit of encounter is
not a style choice but a cognitive limit. Pseudoinefficacy,
from the same laboratory: the felt uselessness of helping one
while millions suffer suppresses helping at all — which an
on-ramp sized to one point is built to answer.

**The adversary's psychology.** The firehose is applied
cognitive science. The illusory truth effect (Hasher):
repetition alone raises judged truth — fluency masquerading as
accuracy; volume is the weapon because repetition is. The
continued influence effect (Lewandowsky): misinformation keeps
shaping inference after correction — the correction economy
loses by design. And manufactured uncontrollability — every
channel contradicting every other — is a learned-helplessness
protocol aimed at whole publics: the product is not belief but
the certainty that nothing can be known and nothing done. §6's
futility finding is this section's mechanisms, weaponised.

Two honesty notes. Psychology's own record includes
replication failures; the mechanisms above are among the
better-replicated, effect sizes vary, and the pattern evidence
in §3–§5 stands independently of any one of them. And
mechanisms are not a control panel: they say which way the
grain runs, not that wood can be made to do anything.

Read back onto §7, the mapping is one line per commitment:
duration works by exposure, contact, and spacing; membership
works by dissonance, commitment, and costly signalling;
demonstration works by reactance avoided and meaning
self-generated; falsifiability is the designer's own debiasing
— the only mechanism in this section aimed at the advocate
rather than the audience.

## Art, marketing, and the border between

Creative advocacy is the phrase under which much of this work
is now commissioned, so the two great carriers deserve their
own examination. The finding up front: neither art nor
marketing is a method. Both are carriers — crafts of making
meaning legible and portable — and like the platforms in §5,
each amplifies whatever unit it is given. Art has carried
memorials and tyrannies; marketing has carried truth and Kony
2012. The unit decides; the carrier delivers.

**What art does that argument cannot.** Art is demonstration's
native language. A made thing shows, and the viewer finishes —
the no-narrator rule was an artistic principle long before it
was an advocacy one. The psychology section explains the
power: transportation lowers counterarguing; images reach
emotion before deliberation; participation in making binds
identity. The lineage of the advocacy image runs in a straight
line: the Brookes diagram (1788); Lewis Hine's child-labour
photographs — he forged nothing, talked his way into the
mills, recorded names and ages, and the laws followed — and
every war photograph since. Participatory art then
institutionalised the audience's completion of meaning: Boal's
Theatre of the Oppressed turned spectators into spect-actors
who stop the scene and play the alternative themselves. Forum
theatre is the audience completing the meaning, performed live
— invented under a dictatorship and exported everywhere.

**The measured limit of the iconic image.** The strongest
natural experiment in this document: Alan Kurdi, September
2015. After the photograph circulated, daily donations to the
Swedish Red Cross Syria fund rose 100-fold — from under 1,000
a day to almost 14,000 — and the average daily amount raised
was 55 times higher; the researchers found the newly created
empathy waned rather quickly. But inside the decay, one number
points the other way: signups for regular monthly donation
rose ten-fold, and by the following January only 0.02% of
those donors had cancelled. The image's burst behaved like
every burst in §4 — and the people it converted into members
stayed. One photograph, both halves of this document's thesis,
measured.

**Art's three failure modes.** Aestheticisation: Sontag's
warning that images of suffering, repeated, first shock and
then anaesthetise — the beautiful photograph of the terrible
thing becomes a way of not acting. Extraction: the subject as
raw material for the artist's object — the war photographer's
oldest wound, and one reason the membership commitment exists.
And service: art is unit-agnostic. Socialist realism was art;
superb craft has carried tyrannies' units before and will
again. Craft guarantees nothing — the Guernica tapestry at the
United Nations was covered over during the 2003 Iraq briefings
precisely because everyone in the building knew what it
argues.

**Marketing: the discipline and the logic.** Marketing entered
advocacy formally in 1971, when Kotler and Zaltman named
social marketing: the advertising toolkit — audience research,
message testing, distinctive assets, disciplined consistency —
applied to causes. The record splits along a line this
document has drawn before. The discipline transfers: truth was
marketing craft of the highest order — researched, tested,
branded, measured — aimed at a demonstration unit, and it
recouped its own cost. The logic does not: marketing's native
logic is transactional — bought reach, quarterly horizon, the
audience as consumers — and applied whole to advocacy it
produces Kony 2012, which was slick-video marketing wearing a
charity's name. Melbourne's Dumb Ways to Die marks the middle:
charming craft, enormous organic reach, and an
operator-claimed 21% drop in near-misses that independent
analysts questioned — the virality was measured better than
the behaviour.

**Brand activism and the border.** Brands now take stances,
and the evidence says stances can sell: Nike's Kaepernick
campaign drew boycott theatre and a 31% jump in online sales
against 17% over the same days the prior year. Read it
precisely: that is commerce aligning with its chosen
customers' values — evidence that positions sell to segments,
not evidence that advertisements change minds. The border case
is Fearless Girl: a bronze of defiance facing the Wall Street
bull, garlanded with the advertising industry's top awards —
commissioned by an index fund to market a fund, by a firm that
later settled claims of underpaying its own women. Art
laundering commerce: every visual power of the medium,
carrying a transaction. The inverse also exists: Patagonia's
owner gave the company away to fund climate work — the
brand-activism gesture done as demonstration rather than
advertisement, an act no advertising budget could buy.

**For makers.** The synthesis: use art as the form and
marketing as the craft, and let neither supply the unit. The
strongest art-advocacy in the record — the quilt, the stones,
forum theatre, participatory portrait projects — is art the
audience or the shown people make themselves: the artwork is a
membership act, not a picture of the cause. Make the thing
people join, not the thing about them; test it like an
advertiser, sign it like an artist, and give it away like
neither.

## 6. The adversary's method — named, not emulated

The contemporary information environment is shaped by what
RAND named the firehose of falsehood: high volume across many
channels, rapid and continuous, no commitment to truth and
none to consistency — with the aim not of persuading but of
entertaining, confusing, and overwhelming. It is industrially
funded; a single flagship outlet has carried a budget around
$300 million per year, before troll farms and proxy sites.

The critical reading: the firehose does not manufacture
belief. It manufactures **futility** — the audience conclusion
that truth is unknowable and engagement pointless. Much of
what looks like Western fatigue is this method's intended
product.

Read as design rather than as content, the firehose is a
damping strategy. Incoherent volume across every channel
raises the audience's internal friction until no signal can
accumulate — manufactured futility is manufactured damping.
Against a damping strategy, more volume is the wrong physics.
The counter is coherence over time, the one thing that still
accumulates when everything else is dissipated; §7 builds on
this reading.

Its structural weakness: it can fake any single image and any
single story, and it cannot fake **duration** — the same
verifiable place, revisited, over years. A lie must be
maintained; a garden only watered. Volume against volume
loses. Time against volume is the asymmetric answer.

The firehose has also learned to buy rather than build.
Influencer marketing grew from a $1.7 billion niche in 2016 to
about $24 billion in 2024, projected toward $32.5 billion in
2025 — an industry whose core commodity is parasocial trust,
the audience's sense of knowing someone. The 2024 Tenet Media
indictment proved that commodity is covertly purchasable by a
state: about $10 million moved through shell companies to one
US content firm, roughly $8.7 million of it to three
commentators alone — one contract at $400,000 per month — with
a state employee editing content undisclosed. The adversary
did not fight the creator economy; it bought shelf space in
it. Hold the two price tags side by side: $400,000 a month for
one rented voice; 120 euros for one permanent stone.

The price-performance curve has since reached its logical end.
In December 2024, Romania's Constitutional Court annulled the
first round of its presidential election after declassified
intelligence alleged a coordinated Russian campaign of
thousands of TikTok and Telegram accounts promoting an
outsider who had declared zero campaign spending and polled
single digits before surging to first place — the first EU
election voided over a platform influence operation, at a
declared campaign cost of nothing. And the curve continues:
synthetic generation is collapsing the cost of fabrication
toward zero, which makes the scarce goods verification,
provenance, and duration. The firehose's endgame is an
environment where nothing is checkable. The durational answer
is a small set of things that always are.

## 7. An alternative — a trimtab method

**A note on the fit.** This is one author's proposal, offered
to be taken, renamed, or discarded. Funders' criteria for
advocacy projects tend to ask the same four things — that
outputs be built so hostile propaganda cannot turn them
against the people shown; that nothing be faked; that the work
not be a one-off action that fades in a week; and that it
break through without a large advertising budget — and the
method answers all four by construction: the capture-design
constraint, the no-fabricated-subjects rule, the duration
commitment, and the trimtab. It dissents on one common
instinct: it is slow where criteria prefer fast delivery — a
dissent argued from the record in §4–§5, not from taste. On
reach it does not dissent: most criteria offer targeted
influence as an explicit alternative to mass reach, and the
method takes that option and names it (the selectivity rule
below). And it adds two things criteria rarely ask for:
membership architecture for the people shown, and
falsifiability beyond a stated theory of change. Any idea can
be read against its funder's criteria this same way — that
reading is part of the point of this section.

**The trimtab.** On a great ship, the rudder is too heavy for
any force to push directly — so a small tab on the rudder's
edge turns the rudder, and the rudder turns the ship (Fuller).
The trimtab principle in advocacy: do not push the audience;
place a small, well-designed thing where the medium's own flow
does the moving. Carriers before crowds. Placement before
volume.

**The question.** The method opens with *what if* — not an
argument, a question. "What if this were possible?" A question
cannot be rebutted, only answered; and the method answers it
by building the thing and letting time verify it. What-if is
the door out of the two comfort zones the field lives in: the
audience's comfort of futility (nothing can be done, so
nothing is asked of me) and the advocate's comfort of argument
(being right is comfortable; showing and staying silent is
not).

**The model beneath the method — tuning, not transmission.**
The four commitments were read off the record. Beneath them
sits a design model, set out at length in the companion case
history on resonance (physics, metaphysics, sociology) and
carried here only as far as its own transfer ledger allows.
The field mostly broadcasts: a message at a frequency chosen
for the sender's convenience, amplitude bought, decay per the
ledger of episodic campaigns. The model reframes the task as
tuning: find the receiver's frequency, place small inputs in
phase, repeat coherently, and let the response build from the
audience's own stored energy — its existing solidarity
structures, memories, calendars, the concepts it already owns.
The trimtab is this model stated as seamanship.

What the model borrows, and at what strength. As *mechanism*,
measured in humans: bodies and groups entrain above a coupling
threshold; synchronised action binds; spaced exposure outlasts
massed exposure (already in the psychology section). As
*structure* — the form of the physics without its mathematics:
the response energy is the receiver's own, released by a
matched input rather than injected by a large one; timing and
placement beat force and volume; accumulation requires inputs
in phase with each other, and incoherent repetition cancels;
damping is always present and sets the ceiling; depth of
response and breadth of audience trade off, and a claim to
both at once is a claim to have repealed a theorem. As *ethic*
— a convergence of traditions two millennia apart, adopted as
commitment and not asserted as fact: the faked stimulus gets
no response; response is invited by condition-making and never
forced, and the attempt to force it produces its opposite;
both parties change, or it was broadcasting with better
manners.

What it does not borrow — and a hostile reader will check:
people are not linear oscillators, and attitudes have no
single clean frequency and no measurable phase; driven
response between an advocate and a distant audience is
analogy, not mechanism — only entrainment in co-presence is
mechanism; no formula computes anything about an audience, and
selectivity is a design choice here, never a number; and the
model's vocabulary arrives pre-captured, by wellness commerce
and by the demagogue's own self-description, so it governs
design and never enters campaign language — the audience
experiences the effect or nothing happened.

**Four commitments**, load-bearing together:

1. **Duration as evidence.** Publish only what can be
   revisited; schedule the revisit into the form. The return
   is the argument. This is also the direct counter to the
   firehose (§6).
2. **Membership, not extraction.** The person shown owns the
   showing. Consent names the reach and the permanence before
   anything publishes; belonging attaches to the contribution;
   withdrawal stays possible. Structure, not compliance.
3. **Demonstration, not persuasion.** No narrator, no ask, no
   concluding caption. The audience completes the meaning or
   nothing happens. Doors, never a hand on the back.
4. **Falsifiability in the room.** State predicted results in
   advance, including expected near-nulls on single exposure;
   run a hostile-read round that can remove content classes
   before they are built; name the failure conditions. A
   method that cannot say what would disprove it is a belief.

Any three without the fourth collapse into something the field
already has: duration without membership is surveillance;
membership without demonstration is a newsletter;
demonstration without falsifiability is faith.

**Three design rules** the model adds, which the four
commitments imply but do not state:

1. **Choose selectivity, and say which.** Depth and breadth
   trade off. A narrow, well-defined audience with a deep,
   accumulating response and a long after-effect is a
   legitimate and usually superior choice to broad reach with
   shallow response and fast decay. Carriers before crowds is
   the narrow choice: tune sharply to the few whose own
   coupling then carries the many. The choice is named per
   campaign, because it also chooses the measuring instrument
   (below); claiming both ends of the trade-off is
   self-deception.

2. **One drop point, in sequence.** Inputs out of phase with
   each other cancel, so the entry point is one and it is
   chosen: one point, one rhythm, proven or falsified, then
   the next. Two images hold the decision — the pebble into
   the pond, where the medium's own coupling carries the wave
   outward from a single entry, and the small hammer on the
   mountain, the same spot returned to blow after patient
   blow. Selection criteria: an existing oscillation (a rhythm
   already running, not still water); strong coupling (a
   carrier whose own audience demonstrably follows them); low
   damping (the moveable middle — those neither hostile nor
   already convinced — not a band already saturated to
   futility); a return path (a medium that structurally
   supports revisit — a newsletter can, a feed cannot);
   survivable placement (consent, safety, and capture design
   travel with the input; a drop point that exposes anyone is
   disqualified whatever its coupling). Candidates for a first
   drop point, in any instance: the room in which the work is
   first shown; a single-carrier test with one listener in the
   chosen audience, the smallest possible pond; and a strand
   of that audience's solidarity infrastructure — twinnings,
   aid groups, congregational networks, teachers, newsletter
   writers. The choice is a named decision with a predicted
   response and a stated failure condition attached — the
   falsifiability commitment applied to placement itself.

3. **Feedback must be able to change the work.** Physics
   leaves the driver unmoved; every human register insists
   that both parties change or it was not response.
   Structurally: what comes back from the audience must be
   able to alter form, subjects, and schedule — or the method
   is transmission wearing better manners. Working with, not
   for, is the two-way condition stated as design.

**The comfort-zone ledger.** The method asks every party to
trade a comfort, and it names the trades openly:

- The **advocate** trades control: no narrator means no final
  word; giving the idea to those it belongs to means no
  ownership; an empty region held empty until real consented
  material exists means patience over polish.
- The **audience** trades the spectator seat: the on-ramp
  (follow, twin, fund, visit) makes the next step theirs, and
  futility stops excusing them the moment one demonstrated
  counter-example exists.
- The **person shown** trades invisibility — only ever by
  their own named, informed, revocable step.

**Design constraints that travel with the method:**

- Every output built to survive hostile re-use; in doubt,
  unpublished.
- Classes of subjects whose exposure cannot be made safe are
  excluded by design — in wartime: children, precise places,
  schedules tied to named people. No gate opens this.
- No fabricated subjects, no composites; scaffolding always
  flagged as scaffolding.
- No trace more durable than the act requires; cryptography
  may touch artifacts, never people.
- The model is unit-agnostic and works for the wrecker: find a
  community's frequency and it is attackable through it with
  small force — a precise description of targeted
  disinformation, which tunes grievance rather than arguing
  anything. So no output hands an adversary a frequency map of
  the community shown: what moves them, when, through whom.
  And declining synchrony is sometimes the design — soldiers
  break step on a bridge.

**Measurement.** The falsifiability commitment requires
instruments, and the model supplies them; all are stated
before the first input.

- *Match the instrument to the chosen selectivity.* A campaign
  tuned to a narrow carrier band will not move a national
  poll, and expecting it to is a category error. Population
  polling measures the terrain; the campaign's own band is
  measured directly. Choosing selectivity chooses the
  instrument, and the choice is declared with the campaign.

- *Terrain instruments — polling that already exists.* Support
  series (Eurobarometer; national trackers), salience
  rankings, willingness-to-bear-cost items, and — closest to
  the blocking belief — futility proxies: expected war
  outcome, perceived efficacy of support. Read, never
  commissioned; they date quickly and are refreshed on the
  same discipline as any audience reading. A small project
  buys no polls.

- *Campaign instruments — four proxies read as curves.*
  Returns to the work are the return rate; follow retention
  after each pulse, plotted as a decay curve, is the
  after-effect; response to pulse N against pulse N−1 for the
  same cohort is accumulation; clustering of returns around
  the pulse schedule is entrainment. Nothing new is collected
  — follows per point, twinning inquiries, funds moved, and
  returns already carry all four quantities once read as
  curves rather than totals.

- *The baseline.* Before the first input: terrain figures at
  T-zero, campaign instruments at their zeros, and the
  predicted curves written down — including the predicted
  near-null on single exposure. Without the baseline there is
  no falsification, only narrative.

- *Benchmarks from the record, not invented.* The burst decay
  curve is the curve to beat: the platform-era spike and
  reversion (§5) and the measured waning after the iconic
  image (the art section). The retention benchmark is that
  same natural experiment's other half — monthly donors
  converted during the burst, of whom almost none cancelled.
  The durational growth benchmark is the stone count: three
  decades, one stone at a time, the price of a dinner per
  point.

- *Publish the nulls.* The deep-canvassing literature
  published its own failure — the 2014 study retracted, the
  2016 replication that held — and came out stronger for it.
  Predicted, observed, and missed are all reported, or the
  falsifiability commitment is decoration.

**A five-part test** for whether something is an instance:

1. a real, revisitable subject;
2. an owner-member, not a source;
3. a form with no narrator;
4. a stated failure condition;
5. the audience's blocking belief named, not assumed.

The historical cases in §3 that pass most of this test —
Stolpersteine, Green Belt, the Madres, TAC — were never
designed from it; they converged on it. What this alternative
adds is deliberateness: choosing in advance what poor
movements discovered under constraint.

## 8. Honest limits

- The method is slow by design and loses every news cycle. It
  is wrong for crisis response and does not pretend otherwise.
- Attribution is unsolved: no method in this document can
  prove the change it causes; this one at least states its
  failure conditions.
- Scale is bounded by consent. Membership architecture does
  not compress; growth moves at the pace of real consent
  conversations. Accepted as a feature — it is also what makes
  the trust legible.
- Under closed, repressive systems, demonstration meets force
  rather than counter-argument, and the cost lands on
  identifiable people. Any audience decision that raises
  personal risk is a deliberate, named decision — never drift.
- The model's mechanism claims are bounded: entrainment is
  mechanism only in co-presence; at distance, everything in
  §7's model is structure and ethic, argued from convergence,
  not measured physics.

- No phase can be measured. The method cannot compute its
  timing; it can only read the audience continuously and
  accept being wrong — which folds back into falsifiability:
  predicted response, observed response, stated failure
  condition.

- Damping may win. Against sufficiently manufactured futility,
  coherence over time is the best available answer and still
  may not accumulate; the method cannot promise otherwise.
